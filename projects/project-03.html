Goals & objectives

1. Maximum profit

The top priority: the highest net profit after costs, and it has to hold up on weeks the rules weren't tuned on.

2. Higher win rate

Among versions with similar profit, prefer the one that wins more often. The target is above 60%.

3. Reasonable trade count

About 1–3 trades a day. Enough to trust the statistics, without overtrading.

Make the strategy testable

Convert discretionary judgment ("strong green bar at a zone") into exact rules, with an audit record explaining every trade taken and every setup rejected.

Find what really works

Identify which individual rules create profit, and improve them without look-ahead bias or curve-fitting.

Validate, then automate

Positive expectancy after costs, win rate above 60%, acceptable drawdown and stable weekly results before building a live system.

The strategy

ZonesDay's open, prior-day high/low/close, and intraday levels confirmed by a second pullback
EntryTouch an established zone and confirm on the same candle: a strong green bar or a long lower wick
RiskStop −0.10, floor +0.20 (−0.15 / +0.30 on wide days); stop to breakeven after a green close
ExitNext zone reached with no more strong signal; hard stop; end of day
NeverBuy the first print of a new low, trade after the cutoff, or use future candles

Research findings

What the data showed

  • My own annotated trades always exited at "next zone + no more strong signal", never at a fixed target. Making that the default exit was the biggest structural change.
  • Re-trading the same level many times in one session was mostly losses and scratches. Capping entries at 2 per zone per day helped (V4).
  • The opening-range zone works as an exit checkpoint but not as an entry trigger (V6).
  • V7's out-of-sample weakness came from one specific bad setup: a wick "bounce" off the day's open that still closes below the open (44% win rate). Rejecting it improved every metric (V7.1).

What was rejected as curve-fitting

  • Widening the wide-day stop looked like a big win (93% win rate) until it turned out to be fitting the exact low of a single historical trade.
  • Moving the entry cutoff to exactly 14:35 added one winner just before one loser. That's noise, not edge.
  • Four different "let winners run" exits all failed. The tight exit is protecting profit on this stock and timeframe.

Current methods

  1. No look-ahead: every decision uses only candles and zones that existed at that moment.
  2. One unified backtester reproduces every version V1–V7.1 on any date range, and gives the same numbers when re-run.
  3. Week-by-week review across the full range, so tuning-window results can't hide out-of-sample weakness.
  4. Root-cause analysis: slicing trades by confirmation type, zone and position relative to the open.
  5. Research log: every rule change is recorded with its hypothesis, result and accept/reject decision.

Next steps

  1. Add new weeks of CNQ data and re-run the week-by-week check on V7.1.
  2. Settle the open entry-window conflict (12:55 vs. 13:55 ET cutoff).
  3. Give "second pullback" a precise numeric definition.
  4. Test the opening-range zone against a placebo zone.
  5. Once results hold across more weeks, start Phase 2: paper trading and automation.

Main challenges

  • Profit target Net +0.42/week vs. a 1.0/week goal. Every lever that closed the gap turned out to be curve-fitting.
  • Regime Choppy weeks (Jul 6–17) stay below 60% win rate for every version.
  • Small sample Only about 6 weeks of backtest range so far.
  • Definitions Some discretionary ideas, like "strong" and "second pullback", still use provisional thresholds.

Lessons learned

My most common discretionary mistake was entering on the first touch of a new low before the zone was confirmed. The rules now forbid it explicitly.

A big jump in results from one parameter is a warning sign. Check trade by trade whether it's a real edge or a single lucky candle.

Save the backtester code with the project. Versions 1–4 had to be rebuilt from prose when the code was lost.

Real annotated trades are the best calibration source. They changed the exit rule more than any parameter sweep did.
