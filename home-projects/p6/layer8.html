How this page works

For a specific address, this page builds a full renovation budget: labor and materials cost ranges for every major scope (structural, electrical/plumbing/HVAC, roof/windows/exterior, kitchen, bathrooms, interior finishes, site/admin), rolled up into three spend-level strategies and a single recommended target budget with contingency. It then checks that purchase price + renovation cost against nearby finished comparable sales, so the renovation number isn't evaluated in isolation from what the finished house could actually be worth.

Built from a property-specific research thread Tony ran and shared, cross-checked this session against independent national cost-data sources (Angi, HomeAdvisor, Fixr) and independently-fetched Sacramento-market sources (GreatBuildz, Parrish Construction, Old Town Electric) — and against the subject property's own live listing. A "check another property" box at the bottom queues the next one.

Read this before budgeting around any number here: these are planning-stage estimates built from published cost guides and one property-specific research pass — not a contractor bid, a structural inspection, or a permit-office valuation. The single biggest source of uncertainty on a stripped-interior property like this one is exactly what condition the framing, slab, sewer line, and any reused systems are in once work starts. Get a structural, electrical, plumbing, HVAC and roofing inspection before treating any total on this page as a number to underwrite against.
Where the numbers come from (click to expand)
  1. Category cost ranges — labor and materials shown separately where the underlying research supported that split; a single installed-cost range otherwise. Every category is tagged to its scope group (structural, systems, exterior, kitchen, bathrooms, interior finishes, site/admin).
  2. Three strategies — Lean/investor, Standard/owner-occupant (the recommended target), and High-end, each with its own total range and a short list of what that spend level actually buys.
  3. Recommended budget — a target line-item list summing to a subtotal, plus a construction contingency (typically 10–15% on a stripped-interior property), giving one recommended all-in number.
  4. ARV reality check — purchase price + renovation budget compared against nearby finished comparable sales, so the renovation isn't priced in a vacuum.
