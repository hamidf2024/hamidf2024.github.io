At a glance
  • Goal: know upfront whether short-term renting, building an ADU, or long-term renting a specific address is even legally allowed — before money is spent on comps or renovation plans that assume a strategy the local ordinance doesn't actually permit.
  • Summary: 5 subject properties scored (3 California, 1 Texas, 1 Arizona), each scored on three separate 100-point rubrics (STR, ADU, LTR eligibility), sourced from the actual county/city/state ordinance and statute text and fee schedules.
  • Inputs: none to start — pick a market tab, or type an address or city into "Check another address or city" to queue a new one.
  • Outputs: three factor-by-factor scorecards per subject (STR/ADU/LTR) with the specific permit fees, limits, and setbacks that apply, plus a 5-city comparison for the Sacramento "live in one unit, STR the other" strategy.

How this page works

Three related but separate questions, so this page has three sections: can I short-term-rent this property (Section 1), can I build and how big an ADU on this lot, and could that ADU itself be short-term-rented (Section 2), and can I rent this out on a standard long-term lease, and what does that legally require (Section 3, added Sept 3, 2026). The first two are set almost entirely locally — a Sacramento County parcel and a City of Sacramento parcel a few blocks apart can answer both questions differently. Long-term rental is more of a mix: the county still governs registration/inspection and zoning, but it's California state law — not the county — that actually caps rent increases, sets eviction rules, and dictates disclosures.

Each subject property gets its own trio of sections scored against the same transparent, published factor rubric, sourced from the actual county/city ordinance text and fee schedules — initiated for 6316 Hillsdale Blvd, Sacramento, CA 95842 (the fixer-upper from Layer 1), joined on Sept 2, 2026 by 437 Forni Rd, Placerville, CA 95667 (the off-market El Dorado County outlier from Layer 1's Sept 2 add), and joined again on Sept 10, 2026 by 8583 Culpepper Dr, Sacramento, CA 95823 (the multi-generational/duplex-style property from Layer 1 with an unresolved second-unit permit question — see that subject's note). A "check another address or city" box at the bottom queues the next one.

Sept 18, 2026 — California / Texas market tabs added, along with this dashboard's first Texas subject: 1505 Sagebrush Dr, Round Rock, TX 78681 (the same address already tracked on Layers 3 and 4). Texas turned out to answer all three of this page's questions very differently from either California county — most notably Section 2, where Round Rock's own zoning code has no legal pathway to an ADU on an ordinary single-family lot at all, a sharp contrast with California's state-mandated ADU right.

Oct 25, 2026 — Arizona market tab added, along with this dashboard's first Arizona subject: 9958 S Margo Dr, Tempe, AZ 85284. Arizona's state-level preemption of both short-term-rental regulation and rent control makes it the lightest-touch jurisdiction this page has scored yet — most notably in Section 2, where Tempe's own guidance gives this dashboard's first confirmed "yes" to short-term-renting a brand-new ADU at all (with one owner-occupancy condition), the opposite of Sacramento County's flat "no" and Round Rock's lack of any ADU pathway whatsoever.

Read this before relying on a score: these are screening scores built from public ordinance text, fee schedules, and this parcel's public zoning/parcel data — not a zoning-clearance letter, a site survey, or legal advice. Setback math assumes the ordinance's standard case; your actual buildable envelope depends on your specific house footprint, easements, and any HOA/CC&Rs, none of which are verified here. Confirm with Sacramento County Planning & Environmental Review (or the relevant city) and a licensed professional before budgeting around any number on this page.
Where the scores come from (click to expand)
  1. STR Score (100 pts) — Legal eligibility of this specific structure/zoning (25), fit with an owner-lives-in-one-unit strategy (20), regulatory cost & complexity (15), rental demand signal (25, cross-referenced against Layer 2's STR comps), and neighborhood/nuisance risk (15).
  2. ADU Score (100 pts) — Zoning eligibility (20), lot size & buildable envelope after setbacks (25), achievable unit size/type (15), cost & fee exposure (15), whether a new ADU here could itself be short-term-rented (15), and permit process speed (10).
  3. LTR Score (100 pts) — Rent control & Tenant Protection Act rent-cap exposure (20), just-cause eviction & single-family-exemption fit (15), county registration & inspection burden (25), security deposit flexibility (10), disclosure & compliance burden (15), and zoning/use eligibility for standard leasing (15).
