At a glance
  • Goal: see what nearby homes are actually earning on Airbnb for a given property, so an STR decision rests on real comparable income evidence instead of a guess — Layer 1's rent figures are long-term-rental based and don't answer this on their own.
  • Summary: 40 subject properties covered (12 California, 20 Texas, 8 Arizona), 153 live Airbnb comps total, each priced off its own 3-month booking calendar.
  • Inputs: none to start — pick a market tab, or type any address into "Check a specific address" to queue a new one.
  • Outputs: occupancy and high/low nightly-rate figures per comp, refreshed against live Airbnb calendars, plus a blended average row per subject property.

How this was built

For each subject property, I searched that exact address on Airbnb (3+ bedrooms, entire home) and identified the nearest 3–4 comparable short-term rentals by map distance. For each comp I read its live booking calendar for the next 3 calendar months to compute occupancy, and priced a "high" (shortest bookable stay) and "low" (longest continuous open stretch) for each month.

Full methodology & edge-case rules
  • Occupancy = only the days actually shown crossed-out/struck-through on the listing's own calendar widget, ÷ bookable days in that month (as of Sep 9, 2026: only days visibly marked unavailable count — not past dates, not checkout-only boundary days, not "blocked by minimum-stay" days that remain otherwise selectable).
  • High price = the shortest bookable stay from the first available check-in day that month (respecting that listing's minimum-stay rule), converted to a per-night rate.
  • Low price = the longest continuous open stretch within that month, converted to a per-night rate — this captures Airbnb's automatic weekly/monthly discounts.
  • Fully booked vs. calendar not released — when a month shows 100% unavailable and the following month is also 100% unavailable, that's treated as the host not having released the calendar yet, not a real booking. Occupancy is still recorded as 100% for that month (it's not bookable either way), but price is estimated as the straight average of the two nearest known real months' high/low figures.
  • Some hosts cap bookings at 28 nights — where that happened, the "low" month price is based on a 28-night stay instead of the full calendar month, noted per comp.
  • Evaluation-window rule (added 2026-09-25): which 3 months get evaluated depends on where today's date falls in the current month. On or before the 20th, the window is the current month plus the next two. After the 20th, the current month is skipped and the window becomes the next three full months instead — e.g. evaluating on Sept 25 uses Oct/Nov/Dec, not Sept/Oct/Nov — since fewer than 10 remaining days of the "current" month isn't a representative sample of that comp's near-term calendar. Applies to every new subject added or refreshed from this date forward; earlier entries on this page keep whatever window was in effect when they were built.
  • Comp distance & approximate location (added 2026-10-01 for Texas, extended to Arizona 2026-10-03): true Haversine straight-line distance computed from each comp's own listing coordinates (not Airbnb's own on-card "X miles away" text, which has repeatedly proven unreliable) to the subject's geocoded address. Airbnb deliberately offsets the map pin it exposes for an unbooked listing by a small random radius for host privacy, so the coordinates used here — and the street-level location shown — are the nearest resolvable point to that offset pin, not a confirmed exact address. Treat the distance figure as accurate and the location text as a general-area reference only. A small number of Arizona comps initially reverse-geocoded to a named point of interest (e.g. a church or clinic) rather than a plain street address; those have the POI name stripped, keeping only the street-level location.
