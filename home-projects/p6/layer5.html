How this scorecard works

A deep-research pass (summarized from a shared ChatGPT investigation, cross-checked here against Zillow's and realtor.com's live agent directories and the California DRE's public license lookup) found the same conclusion every time: no single platform ranks agents by their ability to find off-market deals. Zillow and realtor.com surface production and reviews; RealTrends Verified surfaces objective sales volume; the California DRE confirms a license is real and clean; but "finds properties before anyone else sees them" is a network and business-development skill that has to be pieced together from indirect evidence — team structure, brokerage reach, on-platform service claims, and (ultimately) a direct interview.

So this page scores each candidate across 8 weighted factors instead of a single star rating, and tags every score with how solid the evidence actually is — verified against a second source, strongly evidenced, or single-source only. Off-market ability specifically is scored conservatively for everyone here: none of these candidates had documented, verifiable off-market transaction evidence turned up by this research pass, which is itself the headline finding — it's the hardest thing to verify from public data, and the main reason the direct-interview questions below matter more than the leaderboard order.

Read this before contacting anyone: this ranks candidates on public, verifiable signals (transaction volume in the ZIP, review count/rating, cross-platform presence, active license status). It does not confirm any agent's actual off-market track record — that requires the direct interview questions below. Treat this as a shortlist to interview, not a hire-this-one-first list.

The 10 questions worth asking every candidate (adapted from the source research — the single best filter for off-market ability that no platform can answer for you):

  1. Walk me through 3 off-market or pre-market properties you've sourced for buyers in the last 12–24 months — how you found each, and what happened.
  2. Was each one fully off-market, pre-market, "coming soon," or an office/private listing?
  3. Did you identify the opportunity yourself, or did another agent bring it to you?
  4. How did you know the seller might be willing to sell before it was listed?
  5. What share of your buyer deals in the last 2 years started before the property was public?
  6. How do you proactively search for off-market opportunities?
  7. What relationships do you have with listing agents in this specific ZIP code?
  8. How many agents do you regularly communicate with in this market?
  9. Do you keep a buyer-needs list you distribute to local agents?
  10. What's your plan if nothing suitable is currently listed anywhere?

A vague answer to #10 ("I'll keep an eye on the MLS") is a red flag. A concrete answer (target list, ownership/equity/motivation signals, calls to listing agents, expired/withdrawn listings, direct owner outreach where compliant) is the strongest signal this scorecard can't measure from a directory listing alone.
