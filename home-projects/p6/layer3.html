At a glance
  • Goal: see what truly comparable homes near a subject property actually sold for — real closed sales, not asking prices or a single automated estimate — so its market value and any renovation payback can be checked against hard transaction evidence instead of a guess.
  • Summary: 13 subject properties covered (5 California, 4 Texas, 4 Arizona/Phoenix), 118 verified sold comps total, each priced from its own realtor.com or Redfin Price History log, plus a secondary Zillow off-market check for extra context.
  • Inputs: none to start — pick a market tab, or type any address into "Check another subject property" to queue a new one for a full two-part sweep.
  • Outputs: a ranked sold-comps table per subject (price, $/sqft, distance, and condition where available) plus a secondary Zillow off-market comparison — feeding directly into Layer 1's price assumptions and Layer 8's ARV reality checks.

How this was built (Part 1 — realtor.com)

For the subject address, I opened its realtor.com listing and scrolled to the Home value section's "Nearby home values" widget, then expanded "See more" for the full list of nearby homes realtor.com surfaces there. That list gives current AVM estimates and specs (beds/baths/sqft/lot), not real sold data — so I then visited each nearby home's own realtor.com page individually and read its Property history → Price history section for the actual sold price, sold date, and year built. Only sales from 2020-01-01 to today qualify as comps; everything sold earlier is excluded (shown collapsed below each table for transparency, not counted in the stats).

Full methodology & notes
  • Source list — realtor.com's own "Nearby home values" widget for the subject address, fully expanded via "See more."
  • Per-comp verification — each nearby home's realtor.com detail page was opened separately; sold price/date/year-built come from its Price History log, not the AVM estimate.
  • Qualifying window — sold 2020-01-01 through today. A home with multiple sales in that window uses its most recent sale.
  • Sort order — the comps table is ranked by sold date, most recent first.
  • Two street-name corrections — realtor.com's "Nearby home values" list showed "Coronado Ct" for three nearby homes; that street doesn't exist in Rio Linda. Searching confirmed the correct name is Coronation Ct — all three were found and processed under the corrected name.
  • Part 2 — Zillow neighborhood map — for the subject's Zillow listing, the "Neighborhood: 95673" section shows a map with price bubbles for nearby homes in two colors: yellow "sun" markers (off-market homes, shown with Zillow's own Zestimate or tax-assessed value) and red pins (active/pending listings). Per your instruction, only yellow-marker homes were collected, capped at the 7 closest to the subject. Each was clicked through to its own Zillow page to confirm the exact address, price, specs, and status. These prices are Zillow's estimated values, not realtor.com sold-price data, so they're shown in a separate section below rather than mixed into the Part 1 comps table.
  • Fallback for 6316 Hillsdale Blvd (Sep 5, 2026) — realtor.com's own "Nearby home values" widget returned no results for this specific listing (a genuine site-side data gap, likely tied to low recent-sale turnover on that immediate block). In its place, comps were pulled from a wider realtor.com "recently sold, single-family, 95842" ZIP-wide sweep, with the same per-comp Price History verification as every other subject. This deviation is flagged directly on that subject's card below, not just here.
  • Fallback for 8449 Cutler Way, Sep 12 2026 sweep (superseded Sep 17, 2026) — same "Nearby home values" data gap as 6316 Hillsdale Blvd above; comps were originally pulled from a wider realtor.com "recently sold, single-family, 95828" ZIP-wide sweep. This subject's comps table has since been rebuilt with the tighter, closer, multi-platform methodology described in the next two bullets — see that subject's own card for the current data and its full methodology note.
  • Assessing condition (added Sep 17, 2026, applied so far to 8449 Cutler Way and 8583 Culpepper Dr) — since these homes can't be physically inspected, each comp's condition is read primarily from its own listing's public remarks: explicit renovation language ("renovated," "updated," "turn-key") reads as Updated/renovated or Turn-key/updated; explicit fixer language ("as-is," "bring your vision," "sold strictly as-is") reads as Fixer/needs renovation or, for the most severe cases, Major fixer; "well maintained"/"pride of ownership" language with no update claims reads as Clean original/maintained; a mix of both (e.g. a new HVAC system but sold as-is) reads as Mixed/partly updated or Maintained/standard resale. Where remarks are thin, listing photos and the comp's $/sqft relative to the immediate-area median are used as secondary and tertiary checks. Not a substitute for a physical inspection — a read of what the public listing says about itself.
  • Distance from subject & broader sourcing (added Sep 17, 2026, applied so far to 8449 Cutler Way and 8583 Culpepper Dr) — per your request, sourcing is no longer limited to realtor.com/Zillow: comps are pulled from whichever platform (Redfin, realtor.com, MLS syndicate sites) has the clearest sold record and remarks for that specific address, and each comp's straight-line distance from the subject is computed from geocoded coordinates — not driving directions, which can badly overstate proximity in these cul-de-sac subdivisions (one comp measured 2.5 driving miles via a looping arterial route but only 0.57 straight-line miles away). When this data is present for a subject, its comps table is ranked nearest-to-farthest instead of by sold date, and a separate "nearby active/contingent listings" table appears below the sold comps for context — those haven't closed, so they're never counted in the qualifying-comps stats.
