Project 3: CNQ 5-Minute Zone Day Trading

Can a discretionary "buy the bounce off a zone" habit on CNQ's 5-minute chart be turned into a precise, mechanical rule set? Eight versions were built and backtested without look-ahead bias, each one walk-forward tested and regression-checked against the last. The final version, V7.1, rejects a specific flawed bounce pattern that was dragging down open-zone entries — a single, broad-based fix, not a curve-fit, that improved every headline metric at once.

Owner's goal, in priority order: (1) maximum net profit, and it must hold on weeks the rules weren't tuned on; (2) a higher win rate; (3) a reasonable number of trades (about 1–3/day). By these priorities the current best is V7.1: +2.69 $/share gross, 72.9% win rate, 75 trades over Jul 1 – Aug 14, 2026. Against the stated numeric targets — win rate > 60% and net > 1.0 $/share per week — V7.1 clears the win-rate bar but falls short on weekly net profit (best legitimate pace: 0.42/week), and that gap widens once realistic trading costs are modeled in.

What the research found

Main conclusion

Seven rounds of disciplined, anti-overfitting iteration turned a discretionary habit into a real, repeatable edge — V7.1 earns over 10× the baseline's net profit, with a causal, broad-based fix rather than a curve-fit. But the backtester has never modeled trading costs, and at a realistic 2¢/share round-trip cost, net drops from +2.69 to +1.19 and only two of the eight versions (V7, V7.1) stay profitable at all. The strategy is also structurally weaker in multi-week downtrends — a long-only design fighting the trend — and that weakness is root-caused but not yet fixed.

Method

The entry rule (V7.1)

  1. Zones: the day's open (Zone 1), prior-day high/low/close, and intraday highs/lows — but only once confirmed by a second pullback, never the first print of a new low or high.
  2. Confirmation: price touches an established zone and the same candle confirms — a strong green candle beating the prior red candle, or a long lower wick (at OPEN zones, the wick must also close back above the day's open — the V7.1 fix).
  3. Risk: stop −0.10 / floor +0.20 ($/share; −0.15 / +0.30 on wide days), stop moved to breakeven after a green close.
  4. Exit: next zone reached with no further strong signal; one trade at a time, long only, 09:30–16:00 ET.

How it was tested

  1. Walk-forward, no look-ahead: every decision uses only information available at that candle; trend and zone-strength helpers are computed strictly from prior days' closes.
  2. Regression discipline: every code change was re-checked against V6's known-good output (n=12, 60.0% win rate, PF 3.31) before trusting new results.
  3. Anti-overfitting gate: candidate fixes were tested and rejected when they turned out to be single-trade curve-fitting artifacts (e.g. widening the stop, tuning the entry cutoff), or when they improved one metric while reducing net profit overall.
  4. Reproducibility: one unified backtester (src/bt7_unified_backtester_v1_to_v71.py) reproduces every version, V1 through V7.1, from the same raw CSV.

Root cause, not just tuning

Why V7.1 beats V7

V7's win rate dropped sharply in some weeks. Breaking trades down by entry-confirmation type, zone kind and entry price vs. the day's open isolated the cause: wick-confirmed bounces at the day's-open zone that didn't reclaim the open were systematically weak. Requiring the close to beat the open for that specific case (OPEN-zone wick entries only) fixed it.

Adopted Broad-based (not one trade), causally explicable, improved every headline metric at once — the bar for a real fix here.

Rejected along the way: widening the stop and fine-tuning the entry cutoff — both traced back to a single trade each, not a general effect.

Why June–July lagged

V7 and V7.1 both showed weaker net P&L in June/July. Root cause: a real, multi-week downtrend in CNQ during that window. The strategy is long-only, so during downtrends the average win shrinks and the average loss grows at the same time, even though the win rate itself holds up reasonably well.

Three mechanical fixes were tried and tested honestly:

  • Rejected a trailing trend filter — made results worse
  • Rejected a downtrend stop multiplier — made results worse
  • Shelved a downtrend zone filter — small improvement, but on only 7 trades — too fragile to adopt

Stage 8: could ML beat the hand rules?

Question asked: could a transformer learn this strategy directly from the 11 annotated chart screenshots? Answer: no — 11 images can't train any neural network, vision or otherwise, and the screenshots are a lossy picture of data already held exactly as numbers. Built the viable alternative instead: a gradient-boosted model over the real OHLCV bars plus V7.1's own zone/candle features, trained on May–Jun 2026, tested out of sample on the same Jul 1 – Aug 14 window V7.1's numbers are reported on.

Rejected none of the three ML variants beat V7.1's net profit, the stated #1 priority — so V7.1 stays the live configuration. The ML model matches V7.1's win rate almost exactly but is not a refinement of it: only 5 of its 36 highest-confidence picks overlap with the 95 rule-confirmed candidates in the test window, meaning it learned a different, weaker signal, not a better version of the same one.

“Net $/share” is a cumulative total, not a per-trade or per-day figure: it’s the sum of every trade’s $/share P&L across the whole Jul 1–Aug 14 test window (≈32 trading days) shown in the row’s trade count. Rows with more trades (e.g. 83) aren’t directly comparable to rows with fewer (e.g. 17) on this column alone — profit factor and win rate are the per-trade quality measures; net is the period total. The added “per trade” column divides the total by the trade count so rows are comparable at a glance — by that measure the AND filter is well ahead of V7.1 (0.061 vs. 0.036 $/share per trade), it just hasn’t had enough trades yet to catch up on the total. $100/trade converts this to a dollar amount assuming a fresh $100 position sized at each trade’s own entry price (100 ÷ entry price shares) — same ranking, same conclusion, just in dollars: the AND filter averages $0.134 per $100 trade vs. V7.1’s $0.079.

Kept on file: the "rule AND model agrees" variant's 83.3% win rate and 17.6 profit factor are the best of any version tried in this project, just on too few trades (17) to win on net profit yet. Worth revisiting once more CNQ history (Stage 7.2) gives it room to generate more trades. Validated before trusting any of this: restricting the ML candidate pool to V7.1's own confirmed signals and replaying them reproduced V7.1's official numbers exactly, bar-for-bar, with 0 mismatches. Known limitation found during this stage: this project's zone logic uses sub-cent thresholds (V7.1's entry band is 1.2¢), and cross-checking the two data vendors involved (IEX feed vs. the original consolidated-tape file) showed prices matching closely (median diff ~0.01%, max ~0.46%) but not tick-for-tick — close enough for the rule-based backtest above (which reproduces the official Jul1–Aug14 numbers exactly once spliced), but not close enough for the Stage 8 ML candidate pipeline to reproduce the same window's numbers when rebuilt on the full IEX-sourced history. That re-test is deferred until the candidate pipeline is re-validated against the larger dataset the same way Stage 8 was — not shipped with unverified numbers. Does not hold up after costs at a 1¢/share round-trip cost — half of what the narrow-window table above used as its "high cost" scenario — V7.1 is net negative in 6 of the last 7 years, and negative overall (−8.103 across all 3,605 trades). 2022 is the only year that stays profitable after costs. This is the single most important number on this page for deciding whether V7.1 is tradeable with real money: at realistic retail costs, the honest answer across 6.3 years is mostly no.

Where we're ahead

Fully transparent, hand-auditable rules with an explicit no-look-ahead discipline and a documented, honest root-cause process — including rejected ideas, not just wins.

Where we're behind

No trading costs modeled until now, one stock, 6.4 weeks of data, long only, and no Monte Carlo or deflated-Sharpe correction for the number of versions tried.

What to add

Costs baked into version ranking, a multi-year basket test across similar liquid energy stocks, a relative-volume filter, and a short-side mirror.

Status & roadmap

Progress by phase

Stage 7 — competitive-review upgrades, in priority order

  1. Trading costs in the backtester — commission + spread/slippage per share; re-rank all versions net of cost. Highest priority: can change which version is best.
  2. More history — at least 1–2 years of CNQ 5-minute data, walk-forward by month.
  3. Basket test — run V7.1 unchanged on similar liquid stocks (e.g. SU, CVE, IMO, XOM, CVX).
  4. Relative-volume filter, zone-strength score, short-side mirror, Monte Carlo + Deflated Sharpe, risk-based sizing, live journal — six further upgrades queued behind the above.

Main risks

  • Costs not modeled Every result above is gross; net-of-cost ranking could change the winner.
  • Single stock, short window 6.4 weeks of CNQ is too little to trust as a lasting edge.
  • Downtrend drag Long-only design is structurally weaker in multi-week downtrends; no adopted fix yet.
