Risk rule: two limits on the worst trade, 5% and 15%

Set by the owner on November 23, 2026. A model must carry a stop, and the scoreboard shows which limit it keeps: 5% ($50 on a $1,000 trade) or 15% ($150). A model's levels count, and it is ranked, only inside the 15% limit. 14 of 25 models keep the 5% limit and 23 keep the 15% limit. A price gap through the stop can still make one loss a little larger; the worst trade is shown for every model.

Level 1 reached on G, WR and S, inside the 15% loss limit

M. One-a-month, 15% stop: G1, WR3, S1

  • What it is. Model J with a 15% stop added: one trade a month, sold at +4%, at the stop, or after 60 trading days.
  • How it gets there. 31 of its 35 trades reached +4%. A month with one +4% trade counts toward S, wins toward WR, and lifts the average toward G.
  • What it gives up. The 4 trades that missed were stopped at about 15% each (the worst, 15.7%, gapped through the stop). Four such losses cancel about fifteen winners.
  • It does not reach the monthly-profit goal (GM). With one trade a month sold at +4%, a month’s total reached 5% only once in 35 months.
  • It does not keep the 5% limit. With a 5% stop the same idea (model K) averaged +0.02% per trade.
  • More than $1,000 can be at work: a new trade opens each month while old ones may still be open, up to three at once.
  • The average is not yet proven. Its likely range runs from about −0.4% to +3.5% per trade, and on the 2021–2023 training years it lost money in one year of three (2022). It was the ninth new model run on 2024–2026 in this stage, so it needs confirming on trades after November 2026.

Three things to know before relying on this ranking

  • Few trades. M. One-a-month, 15% stop, E. Peer-Fusion GBM, L. Peer-Fusion GBM, 5% stop, K. One-a-month, 5% stop, H. Peer-Fusion, target and stop made 35, 39, 39, 35, 38 trades in almost three years. With so few trades their averages can change a lot with a handful of results, and one or two large trades can make a month count.
  • Costs. The same-day models make about $0.20 per $1,000 trade. A cost of 1¢ per share is about $0.28 per trade, so they lose money once it is charged. The models that keep a profit after costs are M. One-a-month, 15% stop, E. Peer-Fusion GBM, L. Peer-Fusion GBM, 5% stop, H. Peer-Fusion, target and stop, C. Transformer probe, G. Fusion ensemble, F. Peer-and-oil spring, B. Field-Fusion GBM, D. Fusion gate, J. One-a-month campaign, I. First-passage campaign.
  • Break-even trades. The win rate leaves out trades that end within half a cent of the entry price. The same-day models have many of these: V7.1 percent-scaled wins 70% of decided trades but 46% of all trades. If a break-even trade is not counted as a win, the top five becomes M. One-a-month, 15% stop, E. Peer-Fusion GBM, L. Peer-Fusion GBM, 5% stop, K. One-a-month, 5% stop, H. Peer-Fusion, target and stop.

Same-day models buy and sell within one trading day on 5-minute bars. Multi-day models hold for about 5 days or longer. Under each profit-per-trade bar is the average gross % per trade for 2024, 2025 and 2026 separately. The steady-months figure is the worst of the three years, counting months whose average trade made more than 1%. The small figure beside each win rate counts wins as a share of all trades, break-even ones included. Models with fewer than 15 trades, or that break the 15% loss limit, are scored but not ranked. V1 to V6 use rules set by hand on six weeks of mid-2026, which fall inside the test period, so their scores are flattering.

How it is measured

Fair test

Models were trained and tuned on 2020 to 2023, then frozen and run once on 2024 to 2026. No model was adjusted after its run. A check on November 23, 2026 rebuilt every model input with 2024 to 2026 removed from the data and got identical values, so no input can see the test years. Models I to M were designed after earlier test results were known; that is why the number of looks is stated.

Gross and after costs

The goals are gross, before costs. Robinhood and Schwab charge no commission on CNQ, but each trade still loses a little to the bid-ask spread. The page uses 1¢ per share for that.

Ranking rule

Overall progress is the average of four fractions: profit per trade out of 3%, win rate out of 75%, the share of months whose average trade is above 1%, and the share of months whose total is 5% or more. The rule was fixed before the scores were computed.

Limits of these results

  • One stock. All results are for CNQ only and are backtests, not real trades. Models E onward use consolidated-market prices; earlier models use one exchange's feed (IEX).
  • Few trades for the multi-day models. J. One-a-month campaign has 35 trades in almost three years. Its average of 2.24% per trade has a likely range of +0.3% to +3.9%.
  • The test years have now been looked at 19 times by different models. Each extra look makes a lucky result more likely, so the newest results need confirming on data after November 2026.
  • A rising stock helps long trades. CNQ rose about 39% over the test period, and a randomly timed 5-day purchase made +0.35% on average.
  • Higher levels. A search of 135 settings on the 2021 to 2023 training years, with the test years removed from the data, found none that reaches level 2 on all three goals in every year, and none that reaches level 3. No new model was run on the test years for those levels.
  • 2026 has 11 months of data. Its months are judged on the same fractions (8/12, 10/12, 12/12).
