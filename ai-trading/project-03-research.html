What the early research found (Stages 1–7)

Main conclusion

Seven rounds of disciplined, anti-overfitting iteration turned a discretionary habit into a real, repeatable edge — V7.1 earns over 10× the baseline's net profit, with a causal, broad-based fix rather than a curve-fit. But the backtester has never modeled trading costs, and at a realistic 2¢/share round-trip cost, net drops from +2.69 to +1.19 and only two of the eight versions (V7, V7.1) stay profitable at all. The strategy is also structurally weaker in multi-week downtrends — a long-only design fighting the trend — and that weakness is root-caused but not yet fixed.

Method

The entry rule (V7.1)

  1. Zones: the day's open (Zone 1), prior-day high/low/close, and intraday highs/lows — but only once confirmed by a second pullback, never the first print of a new low or high.
  2. Confirmation: price touches an established zone and the same candle confirms — a strong green candle beating the prior red candle, or a long lower wick (at OPEN zones, the wick must also close back above the day's open — the V7.1 fix).
  3. Risk: stop −0.10 / floor +0.20 ($/share; −0.15 / +0.30 on wide days), stop moved to breakeven after a green close.
  4. Exit: next zone reached with no further strong signal; one trade at a time, long only, 09:30–16:00 ET.

How it was tested

  1. Walk-forward, no look-ahead: every decision uses only information available at that candle; trend and zone-strength helpers are computed strictly from prior days' closes.
  2. Regression discipline: every code change was re-checked against V6's known-good output (n=12, 60.0% win rate, PF 3.31) before trusting new results.
  3. Anti-overfitting gate: candidate fixes were tested and rejected when they turned out to be single-trade curve-fitting artifacts (e.g. widening the stop, tuning the entry cutoff), or when they improved one metric while reducing net profit overall.
  4. Reproducibility: one unified backtester (src/bt7_unified_backtester_v1_to_v71.py) reproduces every version, V1 through V7.1, from the same raw CSV.

Root cause, not just tuning

Why V7.1 beats V7

V7's win rate dropped sharply in some weeks. Breaking trades down by entry-confirmation type, zone kind and entry price vs. the day's open isolated the cause: wick-confirmed bounces at the day's-open zone that didn't reclaim the open were systematically weak. Requiring the close to beat the open for that specific case (OPEN-zone wick entries only) fixed it.

Adopted Broad-based (not one trade), causally explicable, improved every headline metric at once — the bar for a real fix here.

Rejected along the way: widening the stop and fine-tuning the entry cutoff — both traced back to a single trade each, not a general effect.

Why June–July lagged

V7 and V7.1 both showed weaker net P&L in June/July. Root cause: a real, multi-week downtrend in CNQ during that window. The strategy is long-only, so during downtrends the average win shrinks and the average loss grows at the same time, even though the win rate itself holds up reasonably well.

Three mechanical fixes were tried and tested honestly:

  • Rejected a trailing trend filter — made results worse
  • Rejected a downtrend stop multiplier — made results worse
  • Shelved a downtrend zone filter — small improvement, but on only 7 trades — too fragile to adopt

Stage 10: walk-forward training (2020–2023) vs blind test (2024–2026)

Every result above used the hand-picked V1–V7.1 parameters as-is, unchanged, across whatever date range was being tested — including Stage 9's 6.3-year run. That is not the same thing as training on one period and testing on another: nothing above was ever genuinely re-fit on past data alone and then evaluated on data it had never seen. This stage does that properly, for the project's top 3 methods by net profit: V7.1, V7, and Stage 8's best ML variant, Rule OR Model (the hand rule confirms OR the ML model's probability clears a threshold). Train: 2020–2023 (861 trading days). Test: 2024, 2025 and 2026, each touched exactly once, after training was frozen — never used to pick or re-pick anything.

Method — and how overfitting was kept in check

  1. Walk-forward folds inside the training window only: train on 2020 → validate (blind) on 2021; train on 2020–2021 → validate on 2022; train on 2020–2022 → validate on 2023. 2024–2026 are never touched during this step.
  2. Selection by out-of-sample fold performance, not in-sample fit: for V7/V7.1, each fold picks whichever (zone-touch band, entry-cutoff time) combination had the best net-per-trade on that fold's training years — a deliberately narrow 3×3 grid, not a search over per-trade special-case rules (the kind of curve-fitting this project's own lessons-learned doc already flags and rejects). A floor of 30 trades per candidate stops any combination from winning on a lucky handful of trades.
  3. Rule OR Model reuses V7.1's exact, already-validated zone/entry logic (unchanged) to label every zone touch with its would-be P&L, then retrains a small gradient-boosted classifier (identical architecture to Stage 8) on only the fold's training years, with its probability threshold picked on an internal time-split within that training window — the validation/test year is never used to pick the threshold.
  4. Final frozen configuration for each method is selected the same way, using all of 2020–2023 as training, then run once, blind, on each of 2024, 2025 and 2026 — no retuning between years, no peeking.

Gross edge survives blind testing: all three methods stay net-positive, before costs, in all three unseen years, with win rates (57–61%) and profit factors (1.15–1.40) close to what the walk-forward validation folds already predicted — no collapse from training to test. Same cost problem, now under much stricter testing discipline at a 1¢/share round-trip cost, all three walk-forward-trained methods go net negative in 2024 and 2025, and are only marginally positive in 2026 (the one year that was already the cheapest in Stage 9's year-by-year cost table). Training-period (2020–2023, in-sample) Gross % was 0.0415% (V7), 0.0467% (V7.1) and 0.0423% (Rule OR Model) — the blind combined-test Gross % (0.0189%, 0.0204%, 0.0178%) is noticeably lower, as expected for any true held-out test, but not a collapse to zero or negative — consistent with genuine, moderate degradation rather than a badly overfit model falling apart out of sample.

Where we're ahead

Fully transparent, hand-auditable rules with an explicit no-look-ahead discipline and a documented, honest root-cause process — including rejected ideas, not just wins.

Where we're behind

No trading costs modeled until now, one stock, 6.4 weeks of data, long only, and no Monte Carlo or deflated-Sharpe correction for the number of versions tried.

What to add

Costs baked into version ranking, a multi-year basket test across similar liquid energy stocks, a relative-volume filter, and a short-side mirror.

Back-of-envelope: what closing the gap would take

Holding per-trade edge where it is now (~0.02–0.03% gross/trade), reaching 1%/day on CNQ alone would need roughly 35–55 trades/day — 10–20x today's rate. Holding trade count where it is now (~2–5/day), it would need a per-trade edge of roughly 0.2–0.5% — 7–25x anything validated out of sample so far.

Candidate directions for Stage 11

  • Trade frequency as its own research target, not just a by-product of OR-ing methods.
  • Concurrency / multiple open positions.
  • Per-trade edge: tighter entries, better exits.
  • Additional setups beyond the current zone-bounce rule.
  • Basket / multi-symbol expansion (also the top queued Stage 7 item).
  • Short-side mirror, to add trades in downtrends the long-only design currently sits out.

Stage 11 — Target 1 sharpened to per-trade, new candidates tested

Added 2026-11-23. The owner sharpened Target 1 from 1% gross per day to a materially harder bar: more than 1% gross per trade, averaged over 2024–2026, using the same walk-forward discipline (train 2020–2023, blind test 2024–2026).

Feasibility check, done before building anything

CNQ's own daily high–low range in 2024–2026 averages only ~2.0–2.4% of the day's open. A same-day, end-of-day-flattened trade — every method this project has tested so far, V1–V7.1 and Rule OR Model — cannot average more than roughly half the day's own range net of losers, even with perfect entries. An average, net-of-losers return of >1% per trade from a same-day structure is close to mathematically ruled out by the instrument's own volatility at that holding period, not a matter of finding a better rule. CNQ's multi-day swing range is large enough to make >1%/trade plausible instead: the rolling 5-day range averages ~5.5–7.0% of the window's starting price, 10-day ~8.2–10.6%, 20-day ~12.0–15.5%. This is why Stage 11 tested a genuinely different, multi-day holding period rather than another same-day rule variant.

Candidate A — Donchian-channel breakout swing/trend system

A classic, well-documented systematic trend-following design (the “Turtle Trading” / Donchian-channel breakout system): N-day high/low breakout entry, stop at 2×ATR(20), trailing exit on the opposite M-day channel cross, held across multiple days instead of flattened same-day. Walk-forward trained 2020–2023 (narrow 2×2 grid, N∈{20,55}, M∈{10,20}), blind-tested 2024–2026, long-only and long+short variants.

VariantBlind tradesAvg gross %/tradeWin rate
Long-only9−0.05%22.2%
Long+short17−1.59%18.8%

Does not validate a reliably positive edge on CNQ alone over 2024–2026 — but it is the first method tested that is even structurally capable of the magnitude the target requires: individual blind-test trades of +7.55% and +19.43% appear, impossible for any same-day method. With only 9–17 blind trades across 3 years on one symbol, this reads as too small a sample to let the trend-following “big win funds many small losses” mechanism play out reliably — not a disproof of the mechanism.

Candidate B — percent-of-price-scaled V7.1

CNQ's own average price rose ~4.6x from 2020 (~$9.9) to 2026 (~$45.6) in this dataset, while V7.1's stop/target stayed fixed in cents — a concrete, data-grounded reason the existing “Avg gross %/trade” figures could be understating the strategy's real edge in later years. This candidate re-expresses V7.1's already-validated entry/exit rule with a percent-of-entry-price stop/target (each year's reference price = the prior calendar year's average close, no look-ahead) instead of fixed dollars, otherwise unchanged.

MethodBlind tradesAvg gross %/trade
V7.1 original (fixed-dollar)1,5970.0204%
V7.1 percent-scaled (new)1,4110.0205%

Essentially a null result — percent-scaling the offsets does not meaningfully change the per-trade edge, since the actual captured win/loss sizes (driven by the weak-candle early-exit rule, not the nominal stop/target distance) already track price level reasonably well through other channels. A cheap, useful negative finding: closes off a specific, recurring question rather than being wasted effort.

Read plainly: no method this project has built and honestly walk-forward validated — old or new — averages anywhere close to a reliably positive >1%-per-trade return on blind 2024–2026 data. The best positive, high-volume result (percent-scaled V7.1) sits at roughly 1/49th of target. The one method structurally capable of multi-percent single trades (the Donchian swing breakout) does not yet have enough trade volume on one symbol to validate that capability as a reliable, positive average.

Concrete next steps

  • Basket/multi-symbol expansion for the swing system specifically (SU, CVE, IMO, XOM, CVX) — multiplies trade count without changing the per-symbol mechanism, giving walk-forward selection and blind testing enough samples to actually distinguish “this works” from “this got unlucky.”
  • A hybrid: V7.1's proven high-win-rate zone-bounce entries paired with the swing system's multi-day trailing-stop exit, instead of V7.1's own same-day EOD flatten — a genuinely untested combination.
  • Wider ATR stop multiple or an explicit time-stop for the swing system, before concluding the mechanism itself is unviable on CNQ.
  • Re-confirm the exact Target 1 metric with the owner — open questions remain about compounding and multi-symbol account-level returns now that a materially harder bar is in play.

Stage 12 — physics, cross-field ML and a transformer, tested against the per-trade target

Added 2026-11-23. The owner asked for a new, innovative model for the >1% gross-per-trade target, drawing on machine learning (including transformers) and on mathematics, physics and engineering, under the same discipline: walk-forward on 2020–2023, one blind run on 2024–2026.

Result

Target not met. The model chosen by validation lost 0.70% per trade on blind data. The best of the four candidates averaged +0.99% per trade — about 49× V7.1 and the closest this project has come — but on 25 trades, with two trades supplying nearly all of it.

All four are long-only, hold five trading days, enter and exit at the close, one position at a time, no stop or target. “Excess” subtracts what a randomly timed 5-day long earned in the same year (+0.35% on average over 2024–2026, because CNQ rose 38.6%). None of the four averages is statistically distinguishable from zero.

What was built

  • A. Spring-release — a physics rule. A Kalman filter (control engineering) tracks an equilibrium price; the distance below it is treated as a stretched spring (an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck excursion). Buy once the stretch has started to shrink.
  • B. Field-Fusion GBM — a shallow gradient-boosted model over 33 features: Kalman displacement and velocity, OU expected reversion, realised volatility and jumps, permutation entropy, Hurst, rough-path signature terms, and the overnight/intraday split. It scores every day and trades the top of its ranking.
  • C. Transformer probe — a PatchTST-style encoder pretrained on CNQ's own 5-minute bars by reconstructing masked patches, then frozen with a linear probe.
  • D. Fusion gate — meta-labelling: A's signal, taken only when B's model does not disagree.

How it was validated

  • Built on 2020–2023 only (861 days), with folds validating on 2021, 2022 and 2023.
  • Selected on return in excess of a random same-length long, so 2020–2023's steep rise could not pick the winner.
  • Models hash-frozen, then 2024–2026 run once; the script refuses a second run.
  • All 116 blind trades re-checked against the raw file: 0 mismatches. A full re-run rebuilds the frozen models byte for byte.
  • In validation A looked strongest: 27 trades, +3.12% per trade, 78% winners, positive in each year.

Reading this honestly

  • Selected model failed A's entry condition was chosen by trying several versions on the same three years that then “validated” it. Its strong statistics came after that search and overstated the evidence. On blind data its entries kept falling: down 0.86% the next day and 1.0% after three days.
  • Closest so far, not proven B's +0.99% rests on 25 trades. Without its two best trades (+13.1%, +10.3%) the other 23 average +0.06%; the median trade is −0.40%. About 100 trades are needed to tell a true 1% average from zero.
  • What held up B's ranking of days carried over to blind data: its top fifth of days was followed by +1.32% over five days on average, its bottom fifth by −0.35%. And at a five-day hold, trading costs barely matter (+0.97% after 1¢/share), unlike every intraday version above.
  • Transformer Pretraining worked, but the transformer did not beat the simpler tree model — in line with recent published tests of time-series foundation models on returns. More independent data, not a bigger model, is the constraint.

Next steps

  • No retuning on 2024–2026: those years have now been seen by these candidates.
  • Forward-test B exactly as frozen on bars after 2026-11-18.
  • Add peer and crude-oil data (SU, CVE, IMO, XOM, CVX, WTI): about six times the training labels, a blind test on symbols the model has never seen, and roughly 50 trades a year instead of 8.
  • Measure CNQ's stretch against its peers rather than its own past — the setting where mean-reversion theory actually applies.

Data sources identified A review of free sources (2026-11-23) found one that covers 5-minute bars for SU, CVE, IMO, XOM and CVX back to 2020 — Alpaca's free plan, on the consolidated feed rather than the thinner IEX feed used so far — with USO as a time-stamped intraday oil proxy and the U.S. Energy Information Administration's public-domain daily WTI spot series. Collection complete (2026-11-23): 5-minute bars on the consolidated feed for CNQ, SU, CVE, IMO, XOM, CVX, USO and XLE (1,591 trading days each) and the daily WTI series. Checks: no empty or malformed rows, identical calendars, no split errors, and CNQ's daily closes on the new feed within 0.02% of the old file at the median. One defect found: on three days (2021-04-19, 2021-10-25, 2022-03-08) the vendor has no data for five of the eight symbols, so those days are treated as missing. Daily returns correlate with CNQ at 0.84 (CVE), 0.83 (SU), 0.80 (IMO), 0.74 (XOM), 0.73 (CVX) and 0.59 (WTI spot). All price data is kept in the owner's local project folder only and is not published. Details, checks and licence notes: docs/11_Peer_and_Oil_Data_Sources.md.

Stage 13 — peer-and-oil models aimed at the three goals

Added 2026-11-23. The owner asked for new models that reach the first level of each goal, repeating with a new model until they do. This stage adds price data for five peer oil stocks, an oil fund and the sector fund. Same discipline: walk-forward on 2020–2023, one run per model on 2024–2026.

Result

Level 1 reached on all three goals by model J, still to be confirmed. J made +2.24% per trade with an 88.6% win rate and 10, 12 and 9-of-11 qualifying months. Model E, a single model trained on six oil stocks, is the first in the project to clear 1% per trade with a plain 5-day hold: +1.41% over 39 trades, positive in every year.

Why the exit rule had to change

A calculation on the training years showed that the profit goal needs the direction to be right about 61% of the time, and the steady-months goal about 75 to 80% of the time when trades are closed after a fixed number of days. No model here is right more than about 55% of the time. A profit target that is small next to the stock's monthly swing is reached in most trades, so models I and J sell at a target instead.

What J gives up

  • 31 of 35 trades reached +4%. The other four lost 12%, 17%, 0.4% and 17%; one was still open when the data ended.
  • Up to three trades are open at once.
  • Entering on any day with the same exit made about +1.0% per trade in these years. On the training years J's choice of day added nothing.
  • It was the sixth model run on the test years in this stage.

Stage 13, continued — loss limits, a fourth goal, and models K to Q

Added 2026-11-23. During the day the owner added three rules: a limit on the worst trade (5% strict, 15% wide), a fourth goal GM (months per year whose trades add up to 5% or more: 8, 10 or 12 of 12), and a counting rule (trades opened at exactly the same time count as one trade; trades opened at different times count separately). The request each time was the same: train on 2020–2023, test on 2024–2026, repeat until level 1 is reached on all four goals.

Level 1 on all four goals is not reached by any model. Each goal has been reached by some model: M has profit per trade, win rate and steady months; O has win rate and both month goals. The test years have now been used for 22 runs, and the next evidence has to come from new trades or new data.

What was tried on the training years and not run

  • A second, later entry (five or ten days later, on a dip, on a rise): 112 settings, none passed.
  • Stop sizes from 1% to 15% (owner’s request): 1,620 settings, none passed. The widest stop, 15%, did best; stops of 1% to 3% cut the win rate below 50%.
  • Model Q, selling short when the trend is down: 24 settings, none passed; most did worse than always buying.

After model N a new model had to pass the training years with a month to spare, and hold on the five peer stocks, before it could be run on the test years.

What the last runs showed

  • Model P passed every training check and still failed. 96% of its trades won on the training years, 80% on the test years; a 3% target with a 15% stop needs 83% to break even. Checks drawn from rising years cannot test for a slide.
  • Model M is fragile. O buys on M’s signals a few hours later. M had 4 stops in 35 trades, O had 13 in 70. In three months M won on an overnight jump and both of O’s buys, made after it, were stopped.
  • Losses come together. Almost all stops in M, N, O and P were entered between May 2024 and February 2025, while CNQ kept falling.

A combination that shows level 1 on all four goals, and why it is not counted

Put together afterwards from trades already tested, M’s buy at the close plus a second buy at 10:00 the next day shows +1.05% per trade, an 82.9% win rate, S 9 / 10 / 8 and GM 8 / 10 / 8. It is one of seven arrangements of the same buys and the only one that passes. It was picked with the answers in view, the training years chose a different arrangement (model O), and it has no room to spare, so it is not recorded as a result. The table is on the scoreboard page.

The new data improves the month-ahead forecast, and a campaign timed by it beats the same campaign timed by the old forecast, but no setting passes the test for a run on 2024–2026. The count of runs on the test years stays at 22.

Does the information help?

The same model was trained with and without the 51 new features and compared on years it had not seen.

  • One month ahead: the match between forecast and result rises from 0.114 to 0.166 for CNQ and from 0.056 to 0.109 across the six stocks.
  • One week ahead: little or no difference (0.059 to 0.067 for CNQ; 0.054 to 0.029 across the six).
