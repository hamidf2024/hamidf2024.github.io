Where we stand BBB

In plain words

Why single stocks: the Stage AAA models were designed and chosen on SPY and QQQ over 2014–2025, and in the unseen 2026 they did not hold up. These 17 stocks were never used to design or pick a rule, so they are a fresh test of whether the ideas behind the models are real.

What is ready: daily option chains with bid and ask for every trading day of 2017–2025 for all 17 stocks, checked against independent sources.

How the years are used: models are developed and trained (walk-forward) on 2017–2023 only. 2024 and 2025 are kept as unseen test years, opened once for each frozen model. 2026 (to Nov 20) is held back as well.

Focus: pattern recognition with honest, high probabilities of profit, under strict guardrails against too many rules and overfitting (see How we test).

Results so far (Nov 24, 2026): 4 new models tested on the unseen 2024–2025 (Echo $6,601, Line $9,911, Grove $11,795, Chorus $9,993, from $10,000), against $16,695 for holding the 17 stocks. None has passed: four learners on one set of trades and inputs found no edge, so the next step changes the question, not the learner. See progress →

First test (BBB-01): the 12 Top 12 models with their rules unchanged — no setting re-tuned on the stocks. Its exact rules will be written down before it runs.

How we test

Fixed before any result

  • Universe: the 17 stocks, no stock added or dropped after results.
  • Develop and train (walk-forward) on 2017–2023 only.
  • Test once on the unseen 2024–2025, with nothing changed; 2026 sealed.
  • Every experiment written down (pre-registered) before it runs.
  • No setting re-tuned on the stocks in BBB-01.

Costs and risk

  • Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, $0.65 a contract per leg.
  • Every exit priced; held spreads settle at the stock’s close.
  • $10,000 start; at most 5% of the account per position and 50% in total.

What every account reports

  • Goals L (growth), S (steadiness), C (shallow falls), year by year.
  • Against buying and holding the 17 stocks, and SPY.
  • Against the same trades on random days.
  • Then a second data source (QuantConnect) before anything counts.

Focus: pattern recognition with honest probabilities

For each stock and day, a model judges whether a fixed trade (for example a call held 10 days, the matching put, or a put spread) will make money after costs. It looks at about 15–20 inputs fixed in advance: the shape of the option prices (implied volatility, skew, near against far expiries), the stock’s recent path, the market’s fear state, and option activity.

Models, simplest first: analog matching (the most similar past days across all 17 stocks) → a regularised linear model → limited gradient-boosted trees → a small sequence model only if the simpler ones pass. A more flexible model must beat the simpler ones to be used.

Probability: the model gives the chance a trade makes money and trades only above a threshold fixed in advance (for example 70%). The chance must be honest — trades rated 70% must win about 70% of the time on unseen years — and each trade must also have a positive expected value: a high win rate with rare large losses is not accepted.

Guardrails against too many rules and overfitting

Set after Stage AAA, where models built from many rules tuned to 2014–2025 did poorly in the unseen 2026.

  1. Rule budget: at most 3–4 settings per model, fixed in advance; no search for the best value.
  2. Works almost everywhere: a new rule must help in all three development periods (2017–19, 2020–21, 2022–23) and in at least 12 of the 17 stocks.
  3. No cliffs: every setting nudged up and down; a result that breaks under a small change is rejected.
  4. Beats random days: the same trades on random entry days must do clearly worse.
  5. Every attempt counted: each configuration is logged and the results discounted for the number tried; at most 20 per experiment.
  6. Simplest first: single trades before combinations; no sizing tricks.
  7. Beats buy-and-hold of the 17 stocks and SPY on return per unit of worst fall.
  8. Works on unseen stocks too: learning models are also tested on stocks they were not trained on.
  9. One shot at 2024–2025, pass/fail rules fixed before each run, and failures reported as failures.

What the fields agree on

  • Forecast from similar past cases. Weather forecasting, ecology and computer science all do it, and the probability it gives is a plain, checkable frequency.
  • Calibration is its own step in medicine, weather and machine learning: fitted on earlier data, checked with reliability tables and proper scores.
  • Several simple models averaged beat one complex model for probabilities (weather ensembles, epidemic forecast hubs).
  • Early-warning signals are general: rising variance and autocorrelation, health indicators, control charts, change detection and regime probabilities all measure whether a system is drifting into a new state — the “is fear fading or persisting?” question that Stage AAA missed in 2026.
  • Not everything fashionable helps: quantum computing and large time-series transformers bring no clear gain at our scale.

Candidate models (none tested yet)

Each scores the same fixed trades for every stock and day, gives a calibrated probability of profit and an expected value, and trades only above a threshold set in advance. About 18 inputs, fixed before any result: option prices (implied volatility, implied minus realised, skew, term slope), the stock’s path, early-warning measures (rising variance, clustering of big moves, how fast fear fades, regime probability), the market’s fear state, and “surprise” (actual move against the move the options implied).

BBB-05 — Chorus

Run Nov 24, 2026. Every rule, setting and pass mark was written down and saved before any trade result was looked at. Developed walk-forward on 2019–2023 (2017–2018 only warm the inputs and the memory); the unseen 2024–2025 was then opened once. 2026 was not used.

The idea

The average of the three models’ probabilities for every stock, day and trade, with no weights fitted — the way weather and epidemic forecasters combine simple models. Same trading rule and checks. Like Grove, it has no 2019 scores.

Result

Chorus fails all 6 checks. On the unseen 2024–2025, $10,000 became $9,993, against $16,695 for holding the 17 stocks and $14,428 for SPY. On the development years it became $5,264. Usual win rates of the three trades in 2018–2023: call 41.5%, put 29.7%, put credit spread 63.1%.

BBBBBB-02: Echo — a new analog-memory model across the 17 stocks — result

BBB-02 — Echo

Run Nov 24, 2026. Every rule, setting and pass mark was written down and saved before any trade result was looked at. Developed walk-forward on 2019–2023 (2017–2018 only warm the inputs and the memory); the unseen 2024–2025 was then opened once. 2026 was not used.

The idea

For each stock and day, Echo finds the 50 most similar past situations of the same trade across all 17 stocks (15 inputs: option prices, the stock’s path, early-warning measures, the market’s fear) and reads the chance of profit as the share of those past trades that made money, pulled halfway to the usual rate so a thin pattern cannot claim a high chance. Each input is measured against the stock’s own last two years, so one memory serves all 17 stocks. It trades a call, a put or a put credit spread (each held 10 days) only when that chance is at least 65%, at least 5 points above usual, and the past trades made money on average.

Result

Echo fails all 6 checks. On the unseen 2024–2025, $10,000 became $6,601, against $16,695 for holding the 17 stocks and $14,428 for SPY. On the development years it became $1,908. Usual win rates of the three trades in 2018–2023: call 41.5%, put 29.7%, put credit spread 63.1%.

What was learned

  • A high chance of winning is not a good trade. Only put credit spreads (which win about 63% of the time anyway) could ever reach 65%, so Echo became a spread seller: it won 68% of its trades and still lost money, because the average loss was about three times the average win.
