Where we stand BBB

In plain words

Why single stocks: the Stage AAA models were designed and chosen on SPY and QQQ over 2014–2025, and in the unseen 2026 they did not hold up. These 17 stocks were never used to design or pick a rule, so they are a fresh test of whether the ideas behind the models are real.

What is ready: daily option chains with bid and ask for every trading day of 2017–2025 for all 17 stocks, checked against independent sources.

How the years are used: models are developed and trained (walk-forward) on 2017–2023 only. 2024 and 2025 are kept as unseen test years, opened once for each frozen model. 2026 (to Nov 20) is held back as well.

Focus: pattern recognition with honest, high probabilities of profit, under strict guardrails against too many rules and overfitting (see How we test).

Results so far (Nov 25, 2026): 14 experiments, none passed. The Top 12, unchanged (BBB-01): all twelve made money in 2024–2025 ($15,260–$24,386 against $16,695 for holding the stocks), almost all in 2024, but every one fell 40–74% in 2018–2023 — none transfers. Four new learning models made Echo $6,601, Line $9,911, Grove $11,795, Chorus $9,993 on 2024–2025; the fear-fade call trade (Turn) made up to $20,610, no better than calls bought on random days. Built from the parts that carried over (Bedrock), only the ~60-day put spread survived alone: steady but small ($13,114 over 2018–2023, worst fall 15%). Five models from a deep literature search (comparing stocks with each other, and earnings straddles) also failed: 17 stocks are too few for those ideas. Four time-series models (each stock against its own history, Nov 25) failed as well: the trend model’s profit was the rising market. A market-wide stress switch on the put spreads (Sentinel) made them worse, not better. Prism, a new transformer built for this project, learned only the stocks’ upward tilt and did not beat random picks; its redesign on one-week options (Prism 2) did worse, and one fixed correction curve beat both. A third transformer (Helm), trained on the goal to manage the open put spreads, chose never to close any: exits cost more than they save. A probability method that bets only on valid evidence (Wager) found the same upward tilt and made less than simply buying a call every week. See progress →

How we test

Fixed before any result

  • Universe: the 17 stocks, no stock added or dropped after results.
  • Develop and train (walk-forward) on 2017–2023 only.
  • Test once on the unseen 2024–2025, with nothing changed; 2026 sealed.
  • Every experiment written down (pre-registered) before it runs.
  • No setting re-tuned on the stocks in BBB-01.

Costs and risk

  • Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, $0.65 a contract per leg.
  • Every exit priced; held spreads settle at the stock’s close.
  • $10,000 start; at most 5% of the account per position and 50% in total.

What every account reports

  • Goals L (growth), S (steadiness), C (shallow falls), year by year.
  • Against buying and holding the 17 stocks, and SPY.
  • Against the same trades on random days.
  • Then a second data source (QuantConnect) before anything counts.

Focus: pattern recognition with honest probabilities

For each stock and day, a model judges whether a fixed trade (for example a call held 10 days, the matching put, or a put spread) will make money after costs. It looks at about 15–20 inputs fixed in advance: the shape of the option prices (implied volatility, skew, near against far expiries), the stock’s recent path, the market’s fear state, and option activity.

Models, simplest first: analog matching (the most similar past days across all 17 stocks) → a regularised linear model → limited gradient-boosted trees → a small sequence model only if the simpler ones pass. A more flexible model must beat the simpler ones to be used.

Probability: the model gives the chance a trade makes money and trades only above a threshold fixed in advance (for example 70%). The chance must be honest — trades rated 70% must win about 70% of the time on unseen years — and each trade must also have a positive expected value: a high win rate with rare large losses is not accepted.

Guardrails against too many rules and overfitting

Set after Stage AAA, where models built from many rules tuned to 2014–2025 did poorly in the unseen 2026.

  1. Rule budget: at most 3–4 settings per model, fixed in advance; no search for the best value.
  2. Works almost everywhere: a new rule must help in all three development periods (2017–19, 2020–21, 2022–23) and in at least 12 of the 17 stocks.
  3. No cliffs: every setting nudged up and down; a result that breaks under a small change is rejected.
  4. Beats random days: the same trades on random entry days must do clearly worse.
  5. Every attempt counted: each configuration is logged and the results discounted for the number tried; at most 20 per experiment.
  6. Simplest first: single trades before combinations; no sizing tricks.
  7. Beats buy-and-hold of the 17 stocks and SPY on return per unit of worst fall.
  8. Works on unseen stocks too: learning models are also tested on stocks they were not trained on.
  9. One shot at 2024–2025, pass/fail rules fixed before each run, and failures reported as failures.

What the fields agree on

  • Forecast from similar past cases. Weather forecasting, ecology and computer science all do it, and the probability it gives is a plain, checkable frequency.
  • Calibration is its own step in medicine, weather and machine learning: fitted on earlier data, checked with reliability tables and proper scores.
  • Several simple models averaged beat one complex model for probabilities (weather ensembles, epidemic forecast hubs).
  • Early-warning signals are general: rising variance and autocorrelation, health indicators, control charts, change detection and regime probabilities all measure whether a system is drifting into a new state — the “is fear fading or persisting?” question that Stage AAA missed in 2026.
  • Not everything fashionable helps: quantum computing and large time-series transformers bring no clear gain at our scale.

BBBBBB-14: Wager — a probability method: bet only on proven evidence, sized for the worst case — result

BBB-14 — Wager

Run Nov 25, 2026. The method, its settings and the pass marks were saved before any input was built. A first code check exposed a fault in how price scenarios were built (they did not reproduce the market’s own quotes); it was corrected and recorded before any valid result. The method learns week by week from 2017, so there is no separate training period; 2017 is warm-up, 2018–2023 sizes the account, 2024–2025 follows. Neither period is a clean test, because the upward tilt was already known. 2026 not used.

The idea

Not a network this time, but a probability method built from three tools of modern statistics. 1. An evidence meter. Every week, each expired option shows where the stock landed inside the range the market had priced. For every possible correction of the market’s probabilities (289 smooth curves, “the market is right” among them) a running score measures how strongly the outcomes so far contradict it — built so that the conclusion stays valid however often one looks. 2. A believable set. At any day, the curves not yet contradicted form the set of what may be true. 3. A worst-case bet. For each new weekly case the method picks, from six options that can be bought or sold, the position with the best growth under the least favourable curve still believable, after paying the ask, the bid and fees. While “the market is right” is believable the answer is no position; the bet appears only when the evidence allows it and shrinks when the evidence is thin. No threshold, filter or stop is set by hand.

One-week options (expiry 5–9 days ahead), 6,993 cases. Building blocks: sequential calibration tests by e-values (Arnold, Henzi & Ziegel 2023), anytime-valid confidence sequences (Wasserman, Ramdas & Balakrishnan 2020), robust Kelly betting (Sun & Boyd 2018). Their combination on option prices was not found in a search, which is not proof that it does not exist.

Result

Wager fails 4 of its 6 checks. It made money in every two-year block — $13,068 on 2018–2023 and $12,883 on 2024–2025 — but less than both simpler versions of the same bet and less than holding SPY. What it traded was the upward drift of 17 stocks picked with hindsight.

What was learned

  • The evidence meter worked as designed. By January 2018, after half a year of evidence, “the market is right” was rejected: these stocks kept landing higher in their priced range than priced. The believable set narrowed from 289 curves to 5 and never turned back, not even in 2022.
  • What it found is the stocks’ upward drift, nothing subtler. The position was almost always a bought at-the-money call (94% of cases), often paid for by selling a put. That is being long these 17 stocks through options.
  • The careful version lost to the crude one. Buying one call on every stock every week — no model at all — made $19,894 on 2018–2023 and $14,331 on 2024–2025. Kelly sizing without the worst-case step made $18,095 and $13,138. Wager made $13,068 and $12,883. Planning for the least favourable curve made it buy protective puts that cost more than they saved.
  • Why the crude one looks good, and why it does not count: the 17 stocks were chosen in 2026 as today’s large companies, so they are known winners; any long bet on them is flattered by hindsight. “A call every week” was a yardstick here, not a registered model, and it is not promoted to one after the fact.
  • Where this leaves the probability line: the one real regularity in these option prices is the upward tilt, and the most rigorous way to trade it does no better than simply being long. Whether that tilt is skill or hindsight can only be answered on stocks not chosen with hindsight, or on the sealed 2026.

BBBBBB-13: Helm — a transformer that reads the open book and is trained on the goal itself — result

BBB-13 — Helm

Run Nov 25, 2026. The design, training recipe, decision rule and pass marks were saved before any input was built; one change to the training length was recorded before any result. Walk-forward on 2020–2023 (2017–2019 train the first fit); the 2024 fit frozen for 2024–2025, which this trade has already seen. 2026 not used.

The idea

The first two transformers asked whether the market’s probabilities are wrong. Helm asks a different question. The one trade that holds up alone in this stage is the ~60-day put spread: most win a little, a few lose a lot, and the losses come together, which is why it can only be run small. So the open question is not what to enter but which open positions to close, and when. Helm’s tokens are the account’s own open positions — about 100 put spreads across the 17 stocks each day — plus ten snapshots of about 146 large stocks. Every position looks at every other one and at the market, and gets one number: the chance of closing it today. There is no forecast target: Helm is trained directly on the goal, profit divided by drawdown of the account path its own decisions produce. One decision, no stops, filters or thresholds added by hand.

Size: 2 layers, width 32; three training runs averaged. Positions: every ~60-day put spread of BBB-07 (2,249 in 2020–2023, 1,186 in 2024–2025), with daily quotes of both legs. Closing costs the day’s ask and bid plus fees. Related earlier work found in a search: learned exit timing for single index-option positions, deep optimal stopping, and policies trained on Sharpe; the combination here was not found, which is not proof that it does not exist.

Result

Helm fails all 6 of its checks, because it never acts. Trained on the goal, it concluded that no open position was worth closing, in every training and every year. Its account equals holding everything: $11,625 on 2020–2023 and $11,810 on 2024–2025. The highest closing chance it ever gave a position was 0.02%.

The idea

BBB-10 — Sentinel

Run Nov 25, 2026. The research note, rules and pass marks were saved before any sensor value was computed. Judged on 2018–2023; 2024–2025 is already seen for these put spreads and is shown for information. 2026 not used.

BBBBBB-02: Echo — a new analog-memory model across the 17 stocks — result

BBB-02 — Echo

Run Nov 24, 2026. Every rule, setting and pass mark was written down and saved before any trade result was looked at. Developed walk-forward on 2019–2023 (2017–2018 only warm the inputs and the memory); the unseen 2024–2025 was then opened once. 2026 was not used.

The idea

For each stock and day, Echo finds the 50 most similar past situations of the same trade across all 17 stocks (15 inputs: option prices, the stock’s path, early-warning measures, the market’s fear) and reads the chance of profit as the share of those past trades that made money, pulled halfway to the usual rate so a thin pattern cannot claim a high chance. Each input is measured against the stock’s own last two years, so one memory serves all 17 stocks. It trades a call, a put or a put credit spread (each held 10 days) only when that chance is at least 65%, at least 5 points above usual, and the past trades made money on average.

Result

Echo fails all 6 checks. On the unseen 2024–2025, $10,000 became $6,601, against $16,695 for holding the 17 stocks and $14,428 for SPY. On the development years it became $1,908. Usual win rates of the three trades in 2018–2023: call 41.5%, put 29.7%, put credit spread 63.1%.

What was learned

  • A high chance of winning is not a good trade. Only put credit spreads (which win about 63% of the time anyway) could ever reach 65%, so Echo became a spread seller: it won 68% of its trades and still lost money, because the average loss was about three times the average win.
  • The probabilities held in calm years and broke in falling years: trades rated 65% or more won about 67–71% in 2019, 2021, 2023 and 2024, but only 53% in 2020 and 49% in 2022, when losses were largest.
  • Ten-day put spreads lose after costs on these stocks in every period: four legs pay the bid–ask spread twice.
  • The memory does transfer across stocks (leaving each stock out of its own memory changed nothing), but on these inputs it carried no edge: timing was no better than random days.
