Project 9: AI Option Trading

A research-first project to find which machine-learning models and frameworks can trade listed options at a profit after real costs. So far: a reviewed study of 20 models, 20 success factors and 5 frameworks, an audit of a working ML options system, research-grade option data back to 2008, and three daily backtests on SPY and QQQ options — which found a small timing signal but no proven options edge yet. The goal is to grow the whole account after all costs, testing a target of more than 5% a trading day. Win rate is tracked, not chased.

Follow-up tests (Nov 7, 2026)

QuestionAnswer
Is the best model skilled at options, or just at market direction?Market direction. It picks days when SPY/QQQ go on to rise (+0.16% next day vs +0.04% on other days); after removing that move, no extra gain remains.
Do longer holds beat the bid/ask cost?Yes, for every call-buying approach. Holding 5 days: best model +7.9% per trade — but simply always buying a call made +7.4%. The model’s real advantage is a smaller worst drop at a 1-day hold (−4% vs −14%).
What does the model look at?Prefers calls, bought after calm, slightly falling markets: low recent volatility, a small 5-day dip, falling implied volatility, tight spreads.
Does the working system’s network help?No. We rebuilt its network from the screenshots (5 layers × 200 units; gain and tradability outputs; separate calls and puts). On daily data it lost 0.7% per trade at a 1-day hold and made +2.1% at 5 days — below the simple linear model. Its tradability output helped a little.
Does it hold up in 2026?Not enough data to say: only 34–42 trades in the 2026 check.

What this shows

  • Only one model made money: of eight models, only Elastic Net ended above $1,000; LightGBM, the neural network, the rules and a random pick all lost after costs.
  • A small, steady gain at the risk limit: $1,000 → $1,220 over ten years (about 2% a year), while always buying calls fell to $903 and the momentum rule to $550.
  • Bigger bets help only up to a point: 10% per trade reached $9,964 (about 26% a year) but with a 60% fall along the way; 25% per trade ended at $502.
  • Far from the goal: even the best size made about 0.1% a day against the >5%-a-day target.
  • Small accounts can’t follow it: one SPY option costs about $300–800, so with $1,000 a single contract is 30–80% of the account.
  • What the edge is: the model mostly picks days when SPY and QQQ go on to rise; it is market timing expressed through calls, not an options-specific advantage. A rebuilt version of the working system’s network did no better.

What the research found

Main conclusion

No single model wins in options. The best model is the one with a stable edge after costs on data it never saw, not the one with the lowest prediction error.

What matters most (rated 10/10)

  • Data quality, including clean bid/ask quotes.
  • No leakage: expiring contracts, IV derived from prices and stale quotes all leak future information.
  • The right target: IV change, IV–RV gap or probability of a profitable trade, not the next option price.
  • Real costs: a +0.20% forecast is worthless at a 0.35% cost.
  • Out-of-sample testing: walk-forward in time, never random splits, with 2008, 2020 and 2022 tested separately.

A working system, examined

On Oct 29 we reviewed 21 screenshots of a third-party ML options system ("m14.fund3") shown publicly on video. It's the best real-world model of what to build — but none of its returns can be verified.

What it shows

  • A trained, checkpointed neural model (PyTorch-style file, ≈553 million training steps) with versioned runs.
  • The model is scored on gain and tradability, separately for calls and puts.
  • Separate scanner, live ranking, risk view, independent exit schedule and two brokers (Alpaca, Schwab).
  • Displayed realized profit of $53,917 on positions opened Aug 17–27, 2026.

What it doesn't prove

  • No daily return Percentages are on option cost per entry day, not on the account. Account size isn't shown.
  • Concentrated About 85% of the profit ($45,855) came from positions opened on one day.
  • Unreconciled One total shows 7.7% where its own formula gives ≈5.8%; some daily figures changed between snapshots.
  • A training chart value of ≈9.7 is a model metric with unknown units, not a 9.7% daily return.

Displayed realized profit by entry day, Aug 2026 ($)

From the reference system's dashboard, not this project. One entry day accounts for most of the total.

What we copy: the structure — train on real profit after costs, add a tradability output, keep every trade linked to the model version that made it, and run exits and accounting separately from the model.

Tested Nov 7: we rebuilt its network from the screenshots (5 layers × 200 units, gain and tradability outputs, separate calls and puts) and ran it on our daily data. It did not beat our simple linear model (−0.7% per trade at a 1-day hold, +2.1% at 5 days); its tradability output helped a little. Whatever edge the original has likely depends on its unseen intraday data and inputs. See results.

First results: daily version on SPY and QQQ (Nov 7, 2026)

Intraday option prices aren’t available yet, so the experiment was first run on daily decisions: buy one call or put at the close, sell it at the next day’s close, paying the full bid/ask spread and fees. Rules were written down before any result, models were retrained each year (tested 2016–2023), and 2024–2025 was kept sealed and opened once.

Average net return per trade, 2016–2023 (%)

After the spread and fees. Positive means the model made money per trade.

  • One model shows a possible edge: the linear (Elastic Net) model averaged +2.6% per trade in testing and +2.4% in the sealed 2024–2025 period, though that period alone isn’t statistically conclusive.
  • It works as a timing rule for buying calls: on the days it picks, a plain call earned money; on the days it skips, the same call lost.
  • It is small: at the 0.5%-per-trade risk limit the account grows about 1.5–4% a year. The >5%-a-day goal is far out of reach for this design (it made about 0.01% a day).
  • Everything else — simple rules, LightGBM, a small neural network — lost money after costs. The bid/ask spread is the main hurdle on a one-day hold.

The universe: Liquid-150

The 150 underlyings with the most open option contracts, picked from 404 candidates: SPY, QQQ, IWM, DIA and 146 large stocks (49 technology, 26 consumer, 15 finance, 15 health care and more).

Option costs vary widely: the typical gap between bid and ask is 10.6% of the option’s price, from 1.4% on SPY to over 60% on the least liquid names. Costs will be modelled per stock.

Intraday option quotes: sources checked — QuantConnect chosen

Two searches of more than 25 sources (vendors, GitHub, Kaggle, community databases) found these routes:

  • OptionsDX: full option chains every 15 minutes with bid/ask, implied volatility and Greeks, for SPY and QQQ 2016–2023 (30-minute data goes back to 2010), at $5 per year of data — about $80 for both. End-of-day data is free. The vendor doesn’t name its source or guarantee accuracy, so we check it against independent data before use.
  • Databento: the official consolidated exchange feed (OPRA) with 1-minute best bid/ask since 2013, priced by usage, with free starter credit — used to spot-check OptionsDX and, if needed, to cover 2024 onward.
  • QuantConnect (found Nov 7): exchange-grade minute bid/ask bars for US options from 2012 to today, free when the backtest runs in its cloud. Checked Nov 7: in sample weeks from 2013 to 2025 (including the March 2020 crash) there were candidates at almost every 15-minute decision, spreads of about 1–2% (up to 7% in the crash), and a bid for every 60-minute exit. One early-close day (Nov 29, 2019) is missing. A full 2012–2026 run takes about 1–3 hours. This is now the route for the intraday tests.
  • Full quality audit, 2012–2026 (Nov 7): quotes two-sided 99–100% (from 2014), almost never crossed and never below intrinsic value, calls and puts consistent within a few hundredths of a percent, under 0.3% stale quotes in most years, and option and stock moves aligned in the same minute. Monthly quotes match an independent end-of-day file within about 3–4% (snapshot timing). About a dozen whole days are missing after 2016; 2012 lacks weekly expiries. Data currently runs to early August 2026. Verdict: reliable enough for the intraday experiment.
  • ThetaData: every exchange best bid/ask quote from 2016 to today for $80 a month (1-minute from 2020 for $40) — the cheapest copy we can keep locally, but only while subscribed.
  • Alpha Vantage (checked Nov 7): real end-of-day bid/ask chains for any US option since 2008, including 2026, for about $50 for a month — daily only, but it would replace the model-priced exits in our 2026 check with real ones.
  • Also checked and ruled out: Massive’s free options tier (trade bars only, no bid/ask) and two open-source GitHub tools (Alpaca trade bars; India’s NSE end-of-day data).
  • DoltHub (downloaded in full): a free community database of daily option prices. For our 150 underlyings it holds 18.3 million contracts since 2019, about three expiries per stock per day. Its S&P 500 volatility tracks Cboe’s VIX closely (correlation 0.98 over 1,226 days); it covers about 60% of trading days in 2020–23 and every day since 2025, and implausible days are flagged automatically.

Daily data is now covered: end-of-day chains for SPY, QQQ and IWM back to 2008 and SPX back to 2010. Our checks rate them research-grade (quotes on 95–100% of trading days, prices consistent within about 0.2%, S&P 500 volatility tracking VIX at 0.98). That is enough to build and test daily models now. The first experiment’s 15-minute version still needs intraday quotes: OptionsDX 15-minute chains for SPY and QQQ 2016–2023 (about $80), spot-checked against exchange-grade Databento data — or, at no data cost, on QuantConnect’s free exchange-grade minute quotes (2012–today). Alpaca does not sell historical option bid/ask, so it serves as the broker and for recording live quotes from now on.

Where we're ahead

A wider design than any single paper: forecasts, regimes, strategy choice, sizing, a cost gate and risk limits, with institutional benchmarks built in.

Where we're behind

Only daily SPY/QQQ backtests so far, no intraday option data, and no proven edge. The published studies cover more markets and show profits after costs.

Biggest lesson

The published profits come from simple, cross-sectional designs tested net of costs. Reproduce one of those first, then add layers only if they add profit.

Goals & objectives

1. Grow the account

Net growth of total account equity after every cost. Target: more than 5% a trading day on average, compounded, losing days allowed, with 1% and 2% a day as milestones. It's a hypothesis to test, not a promise: 5% a day would multiply the account about 219,000× in a year.

2. Survive

Pass/fail limits on drawdown, daily loss, tail loss and margin. A model that breaks them is out, however profitable.

3. Prove it's real

Beat simple rules and published benchmarks on data the model never saw, under cost stress, with checks for luck and overfitting. Win rate is reported, not ranked on.

Approach

Recommended system: 10 layers

Signals flow top to bottom. Any trade whose expected edge doesn't clear total costs stops at the cost gate.

Validation plan

  1. Statistical: does it predict IV, RV and returns?
  2. Economic: net profit, win rate, drawdown.
  3. Execution: still profitable after spread, slippage, commission and impact?
  4. Crisis: 2008, 2020 and 2022 tested separately.
  5. Walk-forward: train 2010–2018, validate 2019–2020, test 2021, then roll forward a year.
  6. Paper trading before any capital.

Main challenges

  • Data Free data is collected; years of intraday option bid/ask prices still need a paid vendor.
  • Costs Option spreads are wide, and published edges shrink fast as costs rise.
  • Overfitting Every model and setting tried is logged and corrected for before any result counts. Bigger models already overfit on daily data.
  • Edge No options-specific edge found yet: the best model is a modest market-timing signal for buying calls.
  • Target vs risk 5% a day is far above any published result; the risk limits may make it unreachable.
