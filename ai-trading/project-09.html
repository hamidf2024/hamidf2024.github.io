Project 9: AI Option Trading

A research-first project to find which machine-learning models and frameworks can trade listed options at a profit after real costs. So far: a reviewed study of 20 models, 20 success factors and 5 frameworks, an audit of a working ML options system, and a first experiment ready to run. The goal is to grow the whole account after all costs, testing a target of more than 1% a trading day. Win rate is tracked, not chased.

What the research found

Main conclusion

No single model wins in options. The best model is the one with a stable edge after costs on data it never saw, not the one with the lowest prediction error.

What matters most (rated 10/10)

  • Data quality, including clean bid/ask quotes.
  • No leakage: expiring contracts, IV derived from prices and stale quotes all leak future information.
  • The right target: IV change, IV–RV gap or probability of a profitable trade, not the next option price.
  • Real costs: a +0.20% forecast is worthless at a 0.35% cost.
  • Out-of-sample testing: walk-forward in time, never random splits, with 2008, 2020 and 2022 tested separately.

A working system, examined

On Oct 29 we reviewed 21 screenshots of a third-party ML options system ("m14.fund3") shown publicly on video. It's the best real-world model of what to build — but none of its returns can be verified.

What it shows

  • A trained, checkpointed neural model (PyTorch-style file, ≈553 million training steps) with versioned runs.
  • The model is scored on gain and tradability, separately for calls and puts.
  • Separate scanner, live ranking, risk view, independent exit schedule and two brokers (Alpaca, Schwab).
  • Displayed realized profit of $53,917 on positions opened Aug 17–27, 2026.

What it doesn't prove

  • No daily return Percentages are on option cost per entry day, not on the account. Account size isn't shown.
  • Concentrated About 85% of the profit ($45,855) came from positions opened on one day.
  • Unreconciled One total shows 7.7% where its own formula gives ≈5.8%; some daily figures changed between snapshots.
  • A training chart value of ≈9.7 is a model metric with unknown units, not a 9.7% daily return.

What we copy: the structure — train on real profit after costs, add a tradability output, keep every trade linked to the model version that made it, and run exits and accounting separately from the model.

Models, in order

  1. Simple rules and linear model — the baseline every model must beat.
  2. LightGBM — first main model.
  3. Small neural network — with and without a tradability output and call/put outputs.
  4. LSTM or causal CNN — uses recent market history.
  5. Later, only if they add profit: transformers, graph models, reinforcement learning.

Costs decide the winner: Sharpe ratio at different trading costs

Tan, Roberts & Zohren (2024), S&P 100 straddles, 2010–2023. The deep model beats the best rule up to 20 bps and falls behind at 50 bps, unless trained with a turnover penalty.

Where we're ahead

A wider design than any single paper: forecasts, regimes, strategy choice, sizing, a cost gate and risk limits, with institutional benchmarks built in.

Where we're behind

No data, code or backtest yet. Every competitor has at least a decade of tested history.

Biggest lesson

The published profits come from simple, cross-sectional designs tested net of costs. Reproduce one of those first, then add layers only if they add profit.

Goals & objectives

1. Grow the account

Net growth of total account equity after every cost. Target: more than 1% a trading day on average, compounded, losing days allowed. It's a hypothesis to test, not a promise: 1% a day would be about 12× in a year.

2. Survive

Pass/fail limits on drawdown, daily loss, tail loss and margin. A model that breaks them is out, however profitable.

3. Prove it's real

Beat simple rules and published benchmarks on data the model never saw, under cost stress, with checks for luck and overfitting. Win rate is reported, not ranked on.

Approach

Recommended system: 10 layers

Signals flow top to bottom. Any trade whose expected edge doesn't clear total costs stops at the cost gate.

Validation plan

  1. Statistical: does it predict IV, RV and returns?
  2. Economic: net profit, win rate, drawdown.
  3. Execution: still profitable after spread, slippage, commission and impact?
  4. Crisis: 2008, 2020 and 2022 tested separately.
  5. Walk-forward: train 2010–2018, validate 2019–2020, test 2021, then roll forward a year.
  6. Paper trading before any capital.

Benchmark track (Phase 9)

  1. Selection rule: now account growth first, survival limits as gates.
  2. Choose a data vendor and lock a point-in-time dataset.
  3. Rule benchmark: the Goyal–Saretto IV–RV straddle sort.
  4. Reproduce Bali et al.: ML on delta-hedged option returns.
  5. Reproduce Tan et al. with a turnover penalty, at 0–50 bps.
  6. IV baseline, Cboe PUT/BXM comparison, deep hedging later, multiple-testing control, paper trading.

Main challenges

  • Data Option quotes with clean bid/ask history cost money; the vendor isn't chosen yet.
  • Costs Option spreads are wide, and published edges shrink fast as costs rise.
  • Overfitting Every model and setting tried is logged and corrected for before any result counts.
  • Target vs risk 1% a day is far above any published result; the risk limits may make it unreachable.
