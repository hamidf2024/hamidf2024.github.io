Project 5: ML Methods for Intraday Stock Trading

A research-first project to find which machine-learning methods can trade U.S. stocks intraday at a profit. So far: a charter, a 12-phase roadmap, and a pilot that tests four model families on SPY. The goal is the model with the highest net profit, then the highest win rate, with a reasonable number of trades.

What the results mean

  • No model has an edge yet. The only profitable model traded 23 times in 3.4 years, far too few to trust.
  • Before costs, every model was close to zero. A 2 bps round trip is enough to make them all lose.
  • Four different model families failed in the same way, which points to the setup (one very liquid ETF) rather than the algorithm.
  • Published profitable intraday ML ranks many stocks against each other. That's the project's real design and the next step.

Where we're ahead

A strict, leak-free train/test split with no tuning on test data, costs in every result, and overfitting reported openly.

Where we're behind

One ETF instead of hundreds of stocks, 6 features instead of 100+, no random forest or LSTM yet, and data ending in 2021.

Biggest lesson

Every profitable published result ranks many stocks and trades the top vs the bottom. Building that is now the first roadmap item.

Goals & objectives

1. Maximum profit

The top priority: the highest net profit after costs, on years the model was never trained on.

2. Higher win rate

Among models with similar profit, prefer the one that wins more often.

3. Reasonable trade count

Enough trades for the result to be trustworthy, so a handful of lucky trades can't win.

Approach

Guiding principle

Research design (clean data, strict validation, realistic costs) matters more than which algorithm is used. No single ML model has a proven 20-year track record.

Pilot protocol

DataSPY 5-minute bars, 2008–2021
TargetNext 15-minute return; one decision per 15 minutes
Features5/15/30/60-minute momentum, 30-minute volatility, relative volume
Train / testFit once on 2008–2017; test once on 2018–May 2021
Costs2 bps round trip; no trade on low-confidence signals

Target design (from the charter)

  1. Universe: S&P 500 stocks, point-in-time, plus sector ETFs, VIX and futures as context.
  2. Signal: rank stocks by expected 15-minute return vs SPY.
  3. Models: gradient-boosted trees and MLP first; deep models only if they add value.
  4. Portfolio: long the top decile, short the bottom, sector- and beta-neutral.
  5. Validation: walk-forward, purged cross-validation, one final untouched test, then paper trading.

Added from the benchmark review (Phase 12)

  1. Profit-first selection rule with a minimum trade count.
  2. Cross-sectional long-short pilot on S&P 500 stocks.
  3. Reproduce a public benchmark (Qlib + LightGBM).
  4. Add random forest, LightGBM and LSTM.
  5. Cross-stock lagged-return features.
  6. IC, rank IC and multi-seed reporting.
  7. Liquidity tiers: test less-liquid stocks separately.

Main challenges

  • Data Point-in-time S&P 500 intraday data isn't sourced yet; it's the gate to the real design.
  • Edge decay Published intraday edges have shrunk as ML spread through the market.
  • Recency Data ends in May 2021; 2021–2026 still has to be added.

Disclosures

All results are hypothetical backtests on historical data, using a flat $100,000 per trade with no compounding and a 2 bps cost assumption. They aren't a validated strategy, and past results don't predict future returns. Nothing on this page is investment advice.
