Project 9 Research: Every Model We Tested

The research phase in one place: what each model tried, what $10,000 would have become, and what we learned. Every test uses real bid/ask prices and fees, keeps each position within 5% of the account, retrains the model each year and judges it only on years it never saw.

Major goal: grow the account more than 5% a trading day after all costs, with +1% and +2% a day as milestones. That is far beyond anything tested so far, so progress is measured on a ladder of realistic steps: first beat the simple Elastic Net benchmark (L0), then +0.05% a day (L1), +0.10% (L2) and +0.25% (L3).

In plain words

What works so far: a simple linear model (Elastic Net) that buys short-dated SPY/QQQ calls on days it scores well: $10,000 → $51,946 over 2016–2025 at 5% per position (worst drop about −35%). It mostly captures the market’s upward drift.

What we tried: six new transformer designs, intraday trading, single stocks, market timing and selling option premium. Until Experiment 9, none beat it on return and risk together.

First milestone: monthly put spreads run together with Elastic Net beat it on return and risk ($203,352, Sharpe 1.04 vs 0.68), and the spreads stayed profitable on fresh 2026 data. The win came from trading less often and combining unrelated edges.

Bigger positions (Nov 8): each position is now 5% of the account instead of 0.5% (up to 50% at risk). Every gain and loss is 10× larger, so steady strategies grow much faster — Experiment 9 reaches +0.12% a day (L2) — while volatile ones (most single-stock models) are wiped out. Results before Nov 8 were run at 0.5% and are restated here by scaling (approximate).

What holds us back: the bid/ask spread. Most option edges we can find in daily data are about as large as the cost of trading them, so the next rounds trade less often and look for slower, larger edges.

What we have learned

  1. Costs decide almost everything.Buying at the ask and selling at the bid costs 2–10% of an option’s price per round trip, more than most daily signals are worth.
  2. Beat “always long”, not zero.Markets mostly rose in 2016–2025, so many models looked good just by being long.
  3. Profits so far ride the market’s drift.Every profitable buyer-side model loses in the same falls (2018, 2022).
  4. Bigger models are not better here.Transformers and neural nets overfit or learned to stay out; the simple linear model is still the benchmark.
  5. Week-by-week market timing is noise.Learned on/off switches stayed out too often and missed rallies (Experiment 7).
  6. Intraday trading lost the most.Paying the spread every hour wipes out any edge (Experiment 2).
  7. Public stock signals show no skillon large, liquid US stocks in 2021–2025 (Experiment 6).
  8. Check the data before believing a result.A stock-split mismatch once produced a fake profit; every dataset now gets a call–put consistency check.
  9. Picking the top-rated of many noisy options picks the riskiest.Forecasts must be shrunk or the menu kept small (Experiment 8).
  10. Selling options diversifies.Put spreads move independently of the benchmark, but weekly premium barely covers costs; monthly does better.
  11. Change the view, not just the model.The first milestone came from trading monthly and combining unrelated edges (Experiment 9), not from a bigger model.
  12. Monthly stock calls mostly deliver leveraged market drift.LeverSet made +11.9% per trade on intraday quotes, but random stocks made +4% and a simple momentum rule beat the transformer (Experiment 17b).
  13. Weekly multi-leg trades drown in costs.Hedged straddles and butterflies lost money at real quotes in every year (Experiment 17c); the daily version only looked break-even because its exits came from a model.
  14. Price every exit.Experiment 16 looked brilliant (Sharpe 2.6) until we found that trades with a missing closing price had been skipped. With every exit priced (Experiment 18) its +28.6% per trade fell to +3.1% and Sharpe to 0.35.
  15. Freeze the design before trusting a result.SetLift’s gains vanished when its hold period was fixed in advance (Experiment 15: 6 of 9 runs positive, needed 7).
  16. Use the longest history available.Momentum calls looked strong on 2022–2025 but earned about nothing over 2016–2025 (Experiment 17a).
  17. Ranking beats regression for picking trades— but no ranker has passed on unbiased data (Experiments 12, 16, 18 and 19).
  18. The put spread survives real quotes; the daily Elastic Net does not.Re-run on 2 PM intraday prices (Experiment 20a), the monthly put spread still earned +8.2% of its maximum loss per trade with 74% winners, while the Elastic Net leg lost 2.2% per trade and sank the combined account.
  19. Check what the data actually contains.DoltHub’s daily files list only three expiries per stock per day, so most 5-day exits had no quote and had to be estimated (Experiment 22); the graph networks still ranked better than the plain ranker, but the result rests on those estimates.
  20. Slow trades survive real quotes; one-day trades do not.All five Top 10 put-spread accounts failed on 2 PM intraday prices because their one-day Elastic Net leg lost money, while every monthly spread part stayed profitable (Experiment 20).
  21. A small learned gate can help a steady spread.On real quotes, a transformer that skips some months lifted the 0.25-delta spread to a daily-return range clearly above zero, a −18% worst drop and losing years of only −7%, −4% and −1% (Experiment 20c).
  22. Further out of the money is steadier.The 0.25-delta put spread earned less than the charter spread on real quotes but was up in 101 of 120 months with a worst drop of only −24% (Experiment 20b) — the steadiest book so far.
  23. A result that flips on a rerun is noise.The same ranker earned +3.1% per trade in Experiment 18 and −5.5% in Experiment 19 with almost identical data; a graph network over related stocks ranked better but still had only one good year in four.
  24. Steady growth is now a goal.Every model is also judged on months up and losing years; the steadiest so far (Experiment 9, 89 of 120 months up) still had two losing years.
  25. Big positions punish volatility.At 5% per position, steady strategies (Experiment 9) grow ~10× faster, but strategies that win only one trade in three are wiped out by losing streaks.
  26. The risk limits are tight on purpose.Even with up to 50% of the account at risk (charter v1.9), +5% a day would need about +10% a day on the money at risk; the ladder tracks realistic progress.
  27. Check that an option can be priced again later.Saved DoltHub data quotes the same option five snapshots later only 2–42% of the time, so exits had to be estimated; the saved SPY/QQQ/IWM file always can — coverage, not price accuracy, decides whether a dataset can test exits.
  28. Cross-check every dataset against an official series.Comparing 30-day implied volatility with the Cboe VIX exposed broken values (SPY file 2008–09, DoltHub November 2021) that summary statistics hid.
  29. Test the same strategy on two data sources.Experiment 9 (end-of-day) and 20a (2 PM quotes): the monthly put spread agreed closely (month-by-month correlation 0.95), but the one-day Elastic Net earned +0.6% a trade at the close and lost 2.2% at 2 PM — a timing-sensitive model.
  30. Choose the number of underlyings on purpose.Three index funds are cheap, steady and well tested but give few trades tied to the market; 24 stocks add stock-picking at higher cost and risk; 100+ stocks add choice but also lucky results and heavy computing (see the Data section).
  31. Graphs help only against random picks.On real 2 PM quotes the option-chain graph beat random options but not a plain ranker, and its daily promise (Experiment 22) did not survive real exits (Experiment 21).
  32. A small edge does not survive big positions.Redone on the saved file for 2014–2025 at 5% per position, the Elastic Net benchmark kept +0.7% per trade but ended at $10,137: one bad year (−63% in 2022) erased the gains (Experiment 1-F).
  33. Long computer jobs must save as they go.A two-hour model run was stopped by the computing time limit minutes before it finished and lost everything; every long run now saves after each test year and resumes from there.
  34. A weak symbol can drag a good model down.With IWM removed, the same Elastic Net went from $10,137 to $22,933 (Sharpe 0.23 → 0.37, 2022 −63% → −9%): IWM trades lost on average while SPY and QQQ made about +1.3% per trade. Check results per symbol before pooling.
  35. The monthly put spread holds up on the standard.On the saved SPY/QQQ file the monthly charter spread made $34,602 alone (Sharpe 0.80) and $79,167 with the Elastic Net (Sharpe 0.67), both with a daily-return range above zero. The standard roughly halves the earlier Sharpe but keeps the edge.
  36. Learning when to sell premium did not beat always selling it.The learning gates were on almost every month, SideFormer chose put spreads most months, and the weekly learners lost; always-on is the honest benchmark for premium models.
  37. Weekly spreads and weekly volatility trades lose to costs.The weekly put spread ended flat and every straddle/butterfly design lost after costs and hedging; holding a month to expiry keeps more of the premium.

How we test

Two paths, the same five steps

StepPath A: original designPath B: build on existing models
1. Define successNet account return per day after all costs, on years the model never saw; beat simple rules and Elastic Net; stay inside the risk limits
2. BaselineSimple rules, a linear model, gradient boosting and a small standard networkThe same, plus published strategies rebuilt on our data
3. HypothesisA new mechanism aimed at a failure we saw, written down before testingA combination of strengths that should work together
4. Test one changeEach mechanism on and off (ablations)Each borrowed part on and off; vs its sources
5. Refine or change viewKeep what holds across seeds and years; after rounds without progress, change the trade type, horizon or instruments

Unseen years

Models are retrained every year on earlier data only and judged on the following year (walk-forward). Sealed periods are opened once, for frozen models.

Honest costs

Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, plus $0.65 per contract; every result rerun at +25% and +50% wider spreads.

Fair tests

A written plan before each run, 3 random seeds for neural models, a data check on every new dataset, and every result kept, including failures.

What this shows

  • Only one model made money: of eight models, only Elastic Net ended above $10,000; LightGBM, the neural network, the rules and a random pick all lost after costs.
  • A small, steady gain at the risk limit: $10,000 → $12,200 over ten years (about 2% a year), while always buying calls fell to $9,030 and the momentum rule to $5,500.
  • Bigger bets help only up to a point: 10% per trade reached $99,640 (about 26% a year) but with a 60% fall along the way; 25% per trade ended at $5,020.
  • Far from the goal: even the best size made about 0.1% a day against the >5%-a-day target.
  • Small accounts can’t follow it: one SPY option costs about $300–800, so with $1,000 a single contract is 30–80% of the account.
  • What the edge is: the model mostly picks days when SPY and QQQ go on to rise; it is market timing expressed through calls, not an options-specific advantage. A rebuilt version of the working system’s network did no better.
