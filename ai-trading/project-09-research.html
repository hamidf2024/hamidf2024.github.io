Project 9 Research: Every Model We Tested

The research phase in one place: what each model tried, what $10,000 would have become, and what we learned. Every test uses real bid/ask prices and fees, keeps each position within 5% of the account, retrains the model each year and judges it only on years it never saw.

Major goal: grow the account more than 5% a trading day after all costs, with +1% and +2% a day as milestones. That is far beyond anything tested so far, so progress is measured on a ladder of realistic steps: first beat the simple Elastic Net benchmark (L0), then +0.05% a day (L1), +0.10% (L2) and +0.25% (L3).

In plain words

What works so far: a simple linear model (Elastic Net) that buys short-dated SPY/QQQ calls on days it scores well: $10,000 → $51,946 over 2016–2025 at 5% per position (worst drop about −35%). It mostly captures the market’s upward drift.

What we tried: six new transformer designs, intraday trading, single stocks, market timing and selling option premium. Until Experiment 9, none beat it on return and risk together.

First milestone: monthly put spreads run together with Elastic Net beat it on return and risk ($203,352, Sharpe 1.04 vs 0.68), and the spreads stayed profitable on fresh 2026 data. The win came from trading less often and combining unrelated edges.

Bigger positions (Nov 8): each position is now 5% of the account instead of 0.5% (up to 50% at risk). Every gain and loss is 10× larger, so steady strategies grow much faster — Experiment 9 reaches +0.12% a day (L2) — while volatile ones (most single-stock models) are wiped out. Results before Nov 8 were run at 0.5% and are restated here by scaling (approximate).

What holds us back: the bid/ask spread. Most option edges we can find in daily data are about as large as the cost of trading them, so the next rounds trade less often and look for slower, larger edges.

Summary

Six of the top ten share one idea: sell a monthly, loss-capped put spread next to the Elastic Net call/put model (Experiments 9 and 11: #1–3, #5, #6 and #10). It is the only family that reaches L1–L2 over ten years with roughly three months up in four. Transformers on top of it (#3, #6) did not beat simple rules. Single-stock momentum calls (#4) look strong only in 2022–25.

Nothing confirmed is above L3 (+0.25% a day): G1 (+1% a day) is 8× and the goal (+5% a day) 40× the best result.

Steady growth (goal 4)

No model reaches S1 (at least 8 of 12 months up in every year, no losing year) yet. Closest: #3, the transformer gate — no losing year in 2016–2025 (lowest: 2023 +0%, 2025 +1%) and at least 8 of 12 months up in 9 of the 10 years; only 2022 (5 of 12) misses. #2 is similar but lost 3% in 2025. #1 grows fastest but lost money in 2022 (−17%) and 2025 (−5%).

Caveat: choosing the best of 50+ models flatters the winners, and drops are deep at 5% positions — every candidate needs a fresh-data check (paper trading or new months).

Current loop: experiment by experiment (newest first)

Since Nov 7, 2026 the research runs as a loop: learn from every model and from outside research, design a new model, write its test plan before running it, test it on unseen years, publish the result here and record why it worked or not. After rounds without progress, the view of the problem changes (trade type, horizon, instruments), not just the model. Newest first.

Round 13 · Experiment 15Not robust

Experiment 15: Frozen SetLift · same design every year

Idea: stop re-picking the hold period each year; fix it (3, 5 or 10 days) and repeat with 3 training seeds — 9 runs, 2022–2025. Pre-set rule: robust only if at least 7 of 9 runs make money and the middle run earns ≥ +0.02% a day.

Runs that made money

Middle run
+0.006% a day (needed +0.02%)

Why: 3-day holds lost in every seed, 5-day holds (the design chosen in advance) only just broke even, and only the 10-day hold made clear money (+0.02% to +0.06% a day at 0.5% sizing) — choosing it after seeing results would be cherry-picking. At 5% sizing every run falls 72–100% at its worst point. Lesson: SetLift’s past gains came from lucky design choices; it is dropped from the account. A 10-day version could be pre-registered and tested on new data only.

What we have learned

  1. Costs decide almost everything.Buying at the ask and selling at the bid costs 2–10% of an option’s price per round trip, more than most daily signals are worth.
  2. Beat “always long”, not zero.Markets mostly rose in 2016–2025, so many models looked good just by being long.
  3. Profits so far ride the market’s drift.Every profitable buyer-side model loses in the same falls (2018, 2022).
  4. Bigger models are not better here.Transformers and neural nets overfit or learned to stay out; the simple linear model is still the benchmark.
  5. Week-by-week market timing is noise.Learned on/off switches stayed out too often and missed rallies (Experiment 7).
  6. Intraday trading lost the most.Paying the spread every hour wipes out any edge (Experiment 2).
  7. Public stock signals show no skillon large, liquid US stocks in 2021–2025 (Experiment 6).
  8. Check the data before believing a result.A stock-split mismatch once produced a fake profit; every dataset now gets a call–put consistency check.
  9. Picking the top-rated of many noisy options picks the riskiest.Forecasts must be shrunk or the menu kept small (Experiment 8).
  10. Selling options diversifies.Put spreads move independently of the benchmark, but weekly premium barely covers costs; monthly does better.
  11. Change the view, not just the model.The first milestone came from trading monthly and combining unrelated edges (Experiment 9), not from a bigger model.
  12. Monthly stock calls mostly deliver leveraged market drift.LeverSet made +11.9% per trade on intraday quotes, but random stocks made +4% and a simple momentum rule beat the transformer (Experiment 17b).
  13. Weekly multi-leg trades drown in costs.Hedged straddles and butterflies lost money at real quotes in every year (Experiment 17c); the daily version only looked break-even because its exits came from a model.
  14. Price every exit.Experiment 16 looked brilliant (Sharpe 2.6) until we found that trades with a missing closing price had been skipped. With every exit priced (Experiment 18) its +28.6% per trade fell to +3.1% and Sharpe to 0.35.
  15. Freeze the design before trusting a result.SetLift’s gains vanished when its hold period was fixed in advance (Experiment 15: 6 of 9 runs positive, needed 7).
  16. Use the longest history available.Momentum calls looked strong on 2022–2025 but earned about nothing over 2016–2025 (Experiment 17a).
  17. Ranking beats regression for picking trades— but no ranker has yet passed on unbiased data (Experiments 12, 16 and 18).
  18. Steady growth is now a goal.Every model is also judged on months up and losing years; the steadiest so far (Experiment 9, 89 of 120 months up) still had two losing years.
  19. Big positions punish volatility.At 5% per position, steady strategies (Experiment 9) grow ~10× faster, but strategies that win only one trade in three are wiped out by losing streaks.
  20. The risk limits are tight on purpose.Even with up to 50% of the account at risk (charter v1.9), +5% a day would need about +10% a day on the money at risk; the ladder tracks realistic progress.

Unseen years

Models are retrained every year on earlier data only and judged on the following year (walk-forward). Sealed periods are opened once, for frozen models.

Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, plus $0.65 per contract; every result rerun at +25% and +50% wider spreads.

A written plan before each run, 3 random seeds for neural models, a data check on every new dataset, and every result kept, including failures.
