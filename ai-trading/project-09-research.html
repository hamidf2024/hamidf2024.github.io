Project 9 Research: Finding a Better Options Model

A permanent track with one job: find or invent models that move the project toward its goal of growing the account more than 5% a trading day, and test them so strictly that a good result can be trusted. It runs beside the build plan, uses the same data and trade simulator, and only a model that survives every gate moves into the build.

Two paths, the same five steps

StepPath A: original designPath B: build on existing models
1. Define successNet account return per day after all costs, under a fixed trading rule, on data the model never saw; beat simple rules and the strongest existing model; stay inside the risk limits
2. BaselineSimple rules, a linear model, gradient boosting and a small standard transformer; study where they failThe same, plus each listed model run on our data and profiled for strengths and weaknesses
3. HypothesisA new mechanism aimed at a failure we saw, with the result that would disprove it written down firstA combination of strengths that should work together, and why it might not
4. Test one changeEach mechanism on its own, against a same-size model without itEach borrowed part on and off; vs its source models and a simple blend
5. Refine and scaleKeep what holds across seeds and periods, drop what adds nothing, grow only after a gate review; one finalist per path

The test every model faces

Same task

SPY and QQQ options, 7–21 days to expiry, a decision every 15 minutes, a 60-minute hold. The model scores contracts; a fixed rule trades the best one only if its predicted edge clears costs, otherwise it stays out.

Honest fills

Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, plus fees, slippage and delay, tested again at higher costs. Every input is time-stamped so nothing from the future leaks in.

One sealed test

The most recent 12 months of data stay locked until each path has frozen one finalist. They are opened once, then the result is rerun from scratch.

Every run is logged, failures included, and results are adjusted for how many models were tried. A result can be supported, unsupported, inconclusive or invalid.

Path A: first ideas to test

#IdeaWhat would disprove it
A1See the whole option chain at once. Treat every contract as a token so the model compares strikes and expiries instead of scoring each alone.A same-size model that scores contracts one by one does as well.
A2Learn costs and when to sit out. Train directly on profit after the spread, with a built-in “don’t trade” output.Net daily return and trade count don’t improve under the same costs.
A3Adapt memory to volatility. When markets turn fast, rely less on older minutes.The gain doesn’t come from regime changes, or vanishes across periods.
A4Don’t depend on rare big winners. Penalise relying on a few huge trades, for steadier days.Average return falls more than day-to-day swings shrink.

A4 comes from the reference system we reviewed, which made about 85% of its displayed profit from positions opened on one day. Cycle 1 tests at most two of these.

Path B: models to learn from

A starting list, not a ranking. Each is profiled and run on our task before any of its parts are reused.

Proven on option data

  • Gradient-boosted trees on option features: profitable after costs in a 1996–2020 study (Bali et al.)
  • LSTM with a turnover penalty: kept a Sharpe ratio of 0.69 at 50 bps cost vs 0.39 without (Tan et al.)
  • Tree-based volatility forecasts: 13% lower error on one-month implied volatility (Federal Reserve)
  • Surface and chain models (CNN, graph networks) and deep hedging, for later
  • The reference system: separate “gain” and “tradability” outputs — rebuilt and tested Nov 7 (5×200 network): below the linear model on daily data; the tradability output helped a little

General forecasting models

  • TimesFM-3 (Google, 2026): many series at once, gives ranges not just points; non-commercial checkpoint
  • Chronos-2 (Amazon): uses related series and extra inputs without retraining
  • TiRex 2 (NXAI): compact recurrent forecaster
  • Toto 2 (Datadog): not yet independently checked
  • PatchTST and TFT: proven transformer designs

These forecast prices, not option profit, and finance studies show general benchmark rankings don’t carry over. So each is re-ranked on our own test.

What the daily tests taught us (Nov 7, 2026)

  • Every model must beat “always buy a call”, not just zero. Over 2016–2025, simply holding calls for 5 days made +7.4% per trade; our best model made +7.9%.
  • Separate market direction from options skill. The best model’s gain disappeared once the next-day SPY/QQQ move was removed. This check is now part of every test.
  • Bigger models need more data. LightGBM and both neural networks overfit after one pass through ~435,000 daily examples. Larger designs wait for intraday data.
  • Costs set the holding period. Paying the bid/ask spread every day erases most signals; holding 2–5 days pays it less often.

Next

Decide the limits

Account size, drawdown and daily-loss limits, starting broker, data budget, and the research budget for compute and time. These set gate R0.

Get the data

Compare small samples of historical option quotes from two or three vendors, then buy one dataset. The baselines and both paths start as soon as it passes the data check.

Disclosures

Models so far have been tested only on daily, simulated SPY/QQQ data; no result is a proven edge. Figures for other studies are their authors’ own, from different markets, periods and costs. Model names are listed for research only. Nothing here is investment advice.
