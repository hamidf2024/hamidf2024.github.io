Project 9 Research: Every Model We Tested

The research phase in one place: what each model tried, what $10,000 would have become, and what we learned. Every test uses real bid/ask prices and fees, keeps each position within 0.5% of the account, retrains the model each year and judges it only on years it never saw.

Major goal: grow the account more than 5% a trading day after all costs, with +1% and +2% a day as milestones. That is far beyond anything tested so far, so progress is measured on a ladder of realistic steps: first beat the simple Elastic Net benchmark (L0), then +0.05% a day (L1), +0.10% (L2) and +0.25% (L3).

In plain words

What works so far: a simple linear model (Elastic Net) that buys short-dated SPY/QQQ calls on days it scores well: $10,000 → $12,200 over 2016–2025 with small drops. It mostly captures the market’s upward drift.

What we tried: six new transformer designs, intraday trading, single stocks, market timing and selling option premium. Until Experiment 9, none beat it on return and risk together.

First milestone: monthly put spreads run together with Elastic Net beat it on return and risk ($1,455, Sharpe 1.04 vs 0.68), and the spreads stayed profitable on fresh 2026 data. The win came from trading less often and combining unrelated edges.

What holds us back: the bid/ask spread. Most option edges we can find in daily data are about as large as the cost of trading them, so the next rounds trade less often and look for slower, larger edges.

What we have learned

  1. Costs decide almost everything.Buying at the ask and selling at the bid costs 2–10% of an option’s price per round trip, more than most daily signals are worth.
  2. Beat “always long”, not zero.Markets mostly rose in 2016–2025, so many models looked good just by being long.
  3. Profits so far ride the market’s drift.Every profitable buyer-side model loses in the same falls (2018, 2022).
  4. Bigger models are not better here.Transformers and neural nets overfit or learned to stay out; the simple linear model is still the benchmark.
  5. Week-by-week market timing is noise.Learned on/off switches stayed out too often and missed rallies (Experiment 7).
  6. Intraday trading lost the most.Paying the spread every hour wipes out any edge (Experiment 2).
  7. Public stock signals show no skillon large, liquid US stocks in 2021–2025 (Experiment 6).
  8. Check the data before believing a result.A stock-split mismatch once produced a fake profit; every dataset now gets a call–put consistency check.
  9. Picking the top-rated of many noisy options picks the riskiest.Forecasts must be shrunk or the menu kept small (Experiment 8).
  10. Selling options diversifies.Put spreads move independently of the benchmark, but weekly premium barely covers costs; monthly does better.
  11. Change the view, not just the model.The first milestone came from trading monthly and combining unrelated edges (Experiment 9), not from a bigger model.
  12. The risk limits are tight on purpose.With at most 5% of the account at risk, +5% a day would need the options to double every day; the ladder tracks realistic progress.

How we test

Two paths, the same five steps

StepPath A: original designPath B: build on existing models
1. Define successNet account return per day after all costs, on years the model never saw; beat simple rules and Elastic Net; stay inside the risk limits
2. BaselineSimple rules, a linear model, gradient boosting and a small standard networkThe same, plus published strategies rebuilt on our data
3. HypothesisA new mechanism aimed at a failure we saw, written down before testingA combination of strengths that should work together
4. Test one changeEach mechanism on and off (ablations)Each borrowed part on and off; vs its sources
5. Refine or change viewKeep what holds across seeds and years; after rounds without progress, change the trade type, horizon or instruments

Unseen years

Models are retrained every year on earlier data only and judged on the following year (walk-forward). Sealed periods are opened once, for frozen models.

Honest costs

Buy at the ask, sell at the bid, plus $0.65 per contract; every result rerun at +25% and +50% wider spreads.

Fair tests

A written plan before each run, 3 random seeds for neural models, a data check on every new dataset, and every result kept, including failures.

Next

Round 13: freeze one SetLift design (fixed hold, trend/VIX gate, no year-by-year re-selection) and verify it on genuinely new data through a paper-trading test of limit-order fills (plan drafted; needs the owner’s paper account). Meanwhile, look for robust, design-insensitive edges.

Is the best model skilled at options, or just at market direction?Market direction. It picks days when SPY/QQQ go on to rise (+0.16% next day vs +0.04% on other days); after removing that move, no extra gain remains.
Do longer holds beat the bid/ask cost?Yes, for every call-buying approach. Holding 5 days: best model +7.9% per trade — but simply always buying a call made +7.4%. The model’s real advantage is a smaller worst drop at a 1-day hold (−4% vs −14%).
What does the model look at?Prefers calls, bought after calm, slightly falling markets: low recent volatility, a small 5-day dip, falling implied volatility, tight spreads.

What this shows

  • Only one model made money: of eight models, only Elastic Net ended above $10,000; LightGBM, the neural network, the rules and a random pick all lost after costs.
  • A small, steady gain at the risk limit: $10,000 → $12,200 over ten years (about 2% a year), while always buying calls fell to $9,030 and the momentum rule to $5,500.
  • Bigger bets help only up to a point: 10% per trade reached $99,640 (about 26% a year) but with a 60% fall along the way; 25% per trade ended at $5,020.
  • Far from the goal: even the best size made about 0.1% a day against the >5%-a-day target.
  • Small accounts can’t follow it: one SPY option costs about $300–800, so with $1,000 a single contract is 30–80% of the account.
  • What the edge is: the model mostly picks days when SPY and QQQ go on to rise; it is market timing expressed through calls, not an options-specific advantage. A rebuilt version of the working system’s network did no better.

How the experiments work

Narrow on purpose, and fixed before looking at the test period. These are starting assumptions, not settings known to be profitable.

MarketsSPY and QQQ first, then options on the 150 most-traded underlyings

Expiry7–21 days out, delta 0.35–0.60
TimingDecide every 15 minutes, hold 60 minutes, close same day needs intraday data — daily versions (1–5 day holds) run first
FillsBuy at the ask, sell at the bid, plus fees and delay
TargetProfit per contract after costs, in $ and % of premium

Models, in order

  1. Simple rules and linear model — the baseline every model must beat. tested linear model best so far
  2. LightGBM — first main model. tested lost after costs
  3. Small neural network — with and without a tradability output and call/put outputs. tested below the linear model; tradability output helped a little
  4. LSTM or causal CNN — uses recent market history. next, with intraday data
  5. Later, only if they add profit: transformers, graph models, reinforcement learning.
