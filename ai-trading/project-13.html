Project 13: High-Run Stock Day Trading

Can you profit from US stocks that run +20% above the open in the first 15 minutes? Nineteen rule strategies read the first three 5-minute candles and buy at 09:45. Each was frozen and replayed on fresh data, then checked against ten years of history (2016–2025). A gradient-boosting model then tested the opposite trade, shorting the fade.

Major goal: at least 20% return per week, or more than 75% per month, on trading capital. With the whole account in each trade and one trade per morning, that needs about +3.7% net per trade (+2.7% for the monthly goal). Today the best long model averages −0.96% per trade, and even a perfect pick on the current +5%-target trade averages +2.68%, so the research climbs toward it step by step: first break even (M1), then +0.5% (M2), +1% (M3), +2.7% (M4, the monthly goal) and +3.7% per trade (M5).

What the research found

Main conclusion

Buying these stocks at 09:45 has no lasting edge: it lost money in every year from 2016 to 2025, whatever the rules, targets or model. The stocks usually fade after the opening run. A model that shorts the fade made money on weeks it never saw, but the edge is thin and depends on cheap borrowing.

Research phase

New model development · started Nov 6, 2026

Building a new model toward 20% a week

Two research paths, Jan 2025 – Nov 2026 held out as unseen data, and a pass/fail gate at every step. Seven cycles so far, all rejected by their own rules. Five found no edge for long trades at a fixed time: stocks that run at the open drift down all day. The sixth, the Intraday Timing Transformer (A8), asks when to buy during the day instead of which stock at 10:00, and is the best long model so far: −0.18% per trade at 0.5% cost and +0.32% at 0.25% (2019–2024, real fills). A seventh model (A9) that also learns when to sell did worse, because its buy rule favoured volatile stocks early in the morning; its 60-minute signal and its exit rule worked. A9-v2, the first attempt of a continuous loop toward M1, fixed that buy rule and reached −0.03% per trade at 0.5% cost (+0.47% at 0.25%), the best long result so far and just short of break even. Attempt 2, A10, added a second model that judges the transformer's trade proposals and reached milestone M1 on 2019–2024: +0.28% per trade at 0.5% cost (real fills). On the unseen data (Jan 2025 – Nov 2026) it failed (−0.98% per trade): its edge came mostly from thinly traded moments whose recorded prices lag. The loop continues with labels built from real, executable prices.

Method

The daily trade

  1. Screen: common shares and ADRs whose high from 09:30 to 09:44 is at least 20% above the regular-session open, with at least 5,000 shares traded.
  2. Read three candles: 09:30, 09:35 and 09:40 (5-minute bars), with no data after 09:45.
  3. Filter: each strategy's rules accept or reject the stock (blow-off reversal, red inside bar, long wick and so on).
  4. Trade: buy at 09:45, +10% target, −5% stop, out by 10:30; $1,000 per trade, 0.25% cost per side.

How it was tested

  1. Reproduce: an independent replay matched all 174 trade rows from the earlier ChatGPT phase to the cent.
  2. Freeze, then test: each new strategy was frozen before it saw later weeks.
  3. Fresh periods: Oct 2025 – Aug 2026, using exact 1-minute bars from Alpaca.
  4. Ten-year check: 2016 – Sep 2025, delisted stocks included, every year unseen in turn.

Models tried

  • Gradient boosting (about 65 features known at 09:45): no profitable long subset, but it found the short-side edge.
  • Minute and patch transformers, GRU: slightly better at ranking longs (AUC 0.55–0.56), still unprofitable; weaker than gradient boosting on shorts.
  • 15 long target/stop designs (3–20% / 3–10%): all lost money in all 10 years.

With about 25,000 examples, the simpler model wins. Transformers usually need far more data.

Where we're ahead

Fully transparent rules, an exact independent replay, and an honest 10-year test with delisted stocks included.

Where we're behind

Free, delayed data and no real borrow or locate costs. Our finding that these stocks fade matches the research, which also shows the edge shrinking since 2022.

What to add

Paper trading that logs real locate fees and spreads, plus a market-condition filter for weak periods such as Nov 2025 – Feb 2026.

Status & roadmap

Progress by phase

Next steps

  1. Research phase (started Nov 6): new models are developed on two separate paths. Path A designs original mechanisms from scratch; Path B combines the strengths of existing models. Each path follows five steps: define success, build a baseline, form a hypothesis, test one change at a time, refine and scale.
  2. Research results (Nov 7): seven research cycles, then a continuous loop toward the first milestone. Best so far: A10 made +0.28% per trade on 2019–2024 but failed on unseen 2025–2026 data, because its edge came from thinly traded moments with lagging prices. Attempt 3 (A11) trains only on prices that could really be traded. Loop results by model →
  3. Fair tests and gates: a leakage checklist, three scorecards (prediction, trading after costs, operational), at least 3 seeds, and gates G0–G4. Jan 2025 – Nov 2026 is held out as unseen data for every model; development uses 2016–2024 only.
  4. Paper-trade the short model with a broker that has locates (IBKR or TradeZero), recording real fees.

To pass, a design must average at least +0.5% per trade at 0.5% cost per side, be profitable in at least 5 of 6 development years (2019–2024), and stay positive in the week-by-week test on unseen data (Jan 2025 – Nov 2026).

Main risks

  • No long edge 10 years of data show the 09:45 buy loses money.
  • Borrow The short model's profit is mostly in sub-$3 stocks.
  • Costs The edge almost disappears at 0.5% cost per side.
