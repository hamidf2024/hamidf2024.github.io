What the research found

Main conclusion

No stock is close to a sure double. Even the best candidate has under a 50% chance. Doubling in 3 years needs about 26% a year, which the S&P 500 managed in only 3 of 48 three-year windows. The model is far better at spotting stocks likely to halve than ones likely to double.

Model & testing

How it was built

  1. Data: monthly prices for 690 stocks, giving 66,010 historical cases with start dates from 1967 to 2023.
  2. Features: 17 price-history signals: volatility, momentum, drawdown, beta, company age and the market's own trend.
  3. Models: gradient boosting plus logistic regression, calibrated so a "30%" means about 30%.
  4. Walk-forward test: trained only on earlier years, tested on later ones it never saw.
  5. Blend: 50/50 with a rules model built from 3-year scenarios and each company's financial score.

Walk-forward results (unseen years)

Doubling is only weakly predictable (AUC 0.58–0.63; 0.5 is a coin flip). The separate loss model is much stronger (AUC 0.75), so it is used to filter out stocks likely to halve.

Limits: no historical fundamentals in the ML model, and 444 of 1,209 candidate tickers (mostly delisted companies) had no data, which flatters past results.

Where we're ahead

The only one aiming at the owner's actual goal, a 3-year double, with an explicit chance of halving and a thesis built around AI, war and pandemic risk.

Where we're behind

No live track record yet, no historical fundamentals in the ML model, and survivorship bias in the training data.

What to add

Record each pick's Zacks, Seeking Alpha and Danelfin ratings every quarter, and track the Tier A basket against SMH, ITA, BOTZ and the S&P 500.

Status & roadmap

Progress by phase

Next steps

  1. Freeze the Oct 29, 2026 tiers as a paper portfolio and measure it every quarter to Oct 2029.
  2. Re-score after each earnings season and publish a change log.
  3. Add the benchmark checks: rating cross-checks and the ETF baseline.
  4. Improve the model with historical fundamentals and delisted stocks.

Main risks

  • Concentration Categories 1–6 depend on the same AI capex cycle.
  • Geopolitics Taiwan, China/US trade and Korean listings affect several top names.
  • Policy Solar and renewables depend on US tax credits.
